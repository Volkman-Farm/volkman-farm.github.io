# Voice

Read this before writing any post. It supersedes nothing in `BRAND.md`; it corrects how
that document was being applied and adds what it never specified.

Written 2026-09-02 after Albert said the posts did not sound like him.

## Evidence base, and how much to trust it

Three sources agree on most of what follows. Where they agree, follow it. Where only one
source supports something, it is marked.

1. **Albert's own writing.** 725 words pulled from 23 Claude Code session transcripts for
   this project. His typed words about this farm. Thin, and the wrong register (he is
   briefing an assistant, not writing prose), so it proves diction and instinct, not
   paragraph craft. His iMessage history is not on this machine (66 sent messages, 3 over
   80 characters) and Mail.app has no accounts, so those sources came back empty.
2. **Justin Rhodes, the Rooted Man.** Named by Albert 2026-09-02. A men's formation
   newsletter, not a farm blog: fatherhood, faith, historical narrative.
3. **Joel Salatin, the Lunatic Farmer.** Named by Albert 2026-09-02. A food policy and
   liberty blog. Several posts a week, 200 to 1,100 words.

Neither 2 nor 3 is a how-to blog. Both are opinion writers with a persona. That is the
point: **our posts explain, and the writing Albert admires argues or witnesses.**

## The drift to undo

Measured across the 65 posts in `posts/` (~45k words), all Claude-written.

| | Albert | Our posts | What BRAND.md actually says |
|---|---|---|---|
| Contractions per 1k words | 19.12 | **0.02** | nothing; its own prose uses them |
| Numerals per 1k words | 44.12 | **1.76** | "Numerals when informational" |

The archive holds roughly **one contraction in 45,000 words**. No rule ever asked for
that. BRAND.md section 4 writes "when something didn't work" and "we don't curate" in its
own voice. The formality was invented and then held for two months.

Numerals ran the same way in reverse. BRAND.md says use them; the posts spelled almost
everything out. Albert writes "3 chickens died," "18inches," "50lb bag," "2-3 days,"
"about 3 inches."

**Use contractions freely. Use numerals for anything informational.** Neither is a new
voice. Both are compliance with a document that already existed.

## Banned mannerisms

These are Claude's, not Albert's, not anyone's. Counts are posts out of 65 containing them.

- `Here is` / `Here's the` as a paragraph opener (56)
- `actually` (48)
- `honest`, `honestly`, `the honest part` (38)
- `rather than` as the default contrast (29)
- A sentence opening `That is` (31)
- **`That is the whole idea / answer / thing`** (17). Worst offender.

The last one names the real habit: **summarizing your own point.** In 55 sentences of
Albert's writing it happens zero times. He states a fact and stops. Rhodes lets a thesis
emerge late. Salatin asserts and moves. State the thing, then go on to the next thing.
Do not tell the reader what the thing meant.

## Person

Default stays "we." BRAND.md is right that this is a family operation.

**But "I" is now allowed, sparingly, where a specific human did a specific thing.** Albert
writes "i" for his own work and "we" for the family's. Rhodes and Salatin both write in
first person singular constantly. Across 65 posts ours has never once said "I," and that
is a rule nobody wrote.

Use it when Albert chopped the sunn hemp, built the shed, made the call. Not as a general
register. Never "the Volkman family" in the third person.

**Do not literalize Albert's casing.** Zero of 55 sentences in his corpus start with a
capital and he never writes "I" uppercase. That is texting convention. Posts stay
sentence-cased with normal capitals.

## Sentences and structure

- **Vary the rhythm hard.** Salatin puts a one-word sentence next to a multi-clause one.
  Ours: mean 15.5 words, stdev 9.3, only 3.8% of sentences three words or shorter. Break
  more. Let a short one land alone.
- **Single-sentence paragraphs carry weight.** Use them deliberately, not decoratively.
- **Subheads are not a quota, and they are not the default.** 58 of our 65 posts have
  them, median 3. Both Salatin posts reviewed have **zero**, at 700 and 1,100 words. A
  post under about 600 words rarely needs one. Never space them on a cadence.
- **Concrete nouns do the persuading.** Salatin: number-two pencils, a spiral notebook, a
  writing cabin on stilts. Albert: Fox Farm Happy Frog, ZeroTol HC, Kangen, True Leaf,
  Nazca. Name the actual product. BRAND.md already demanded this ("specifics, not
  adjectives") and the posts went generic instead.
- **Contingency goes in parentheses, not into a hedging sentence.** Albert: "(depending on
  how hard it rains)", "(and it'll be helpful when we introduce new chicks)". One post
  paragraph of hedging can usually become four words in parens.
- **`so` is the workhorse connector.** Albert uses it at 4.41/1k against our 1.59/1k.

## Openings and closings

**Open on a scene or a fact, not on the subject.** Rhodes opens on three boys crossing the
Appalachians before he says what the piece is about. Salatin opens on the woman being
investigated. Our posts open by naming the topic, which is the encyclopedia move. "The word
is guild" is the thing to stop doing.

**Close by handing the reader a job, not a takeaway.** This is the single strongest
convergence in all three sources. Albert, on the arborist note: *the article should have
the reader do the exercise.* Rhodes closes on a direct challenge. Salatin closes on a
question that reframes what came before. Our posts close on a summary and a CTA.

Still at most one CTA, and many posts want none.

## Opinion

State it flat. Albert corrects with "untrue," "no," "wait, i thought we had already done a
cilantro post?" Salatin calls Berry a giant without qualifying it. Our posts hedge by
reflex, and much of that hedging is habit rather than the regulatory caution it imitates.

Be comfortable with imprecision without defending it. Albert on the seeding rate: *it was
the bag we had and it was close enough to the amount needed for the area.* No apology, no
framing. That sentence is the whole register.

The hedging that stays mandatory is nutrition and health language only. See below.

## What we refuse to take from Rhodes and Salatin

Their voices are welded to personas that sell those personas. Ours sells greens.

- **Salatin's anti-regulatory crusade.** He writes against inspectors and agencies. We grow
  under Florida rules and this blog already carries hard guardrails about post-harvest
  handling. Opinions about scale, quality, chickens, and what a tray is worth: yes.
  Opinions about the department of agriculture: never.
- **Rhodes's faith and masculinity frame.** Wrong audience, wrong product.
- **Sustained second-person imperative.** Rhodes commands readers who subscribed to be
  commanded. Ours arrive from a search deciding whether to spend $3.50 an ounce.
- **Rhetorical questions as an engine.** Fine once. Not as the drive.
- **Flourishes.** Salatin's "bureaucracy beast" is precisely the ornament BRAND.md bans.
- **Provocation headlines.** Titles stay descriptive and sentence case.

## Register by pillar

The point of view is not evenly distributed.

- **Homestead journal, Permaculture in practice.** These carry the voice. Longest and
  loosest already (medians 803 and 850 words). First person allowed, subheads optional,
  open cold, allowed to end on a question or an unresolved thing.
- **On the route.** Middle. Opinionated about how we work, plainer about mechanics.
- **Variety spotlight, Growing notes, In the kitchen, Reader questions.** Stay
  informational. These do the commercial and search work. Tighter, more structured, and a
  customer choosing greens does not want a sermon. Reader questions are already the
  shortest at 463 words; keep them that way.

## Unchanged

Every existing hard rule in `BRAND.md` and the guardrails section of `CONTENT-PLAN.md`
still binds. Nothing here loosens them:

- No em-dashes, anywhere.
- No post-harvest washing, rinsing, drying, or processing claims. Florida regulation.
- Nutrition claims hedged: "carries," "is high in," "research suggests."
- No invented customers, demand, drops, regulars, or waiting lists.
- No gator, German, or Bavarian references.
- Kids: no names, no ages, no faces. Birth-order and role only.
- The banned word list in BRAND.md section 4 stands in full.
