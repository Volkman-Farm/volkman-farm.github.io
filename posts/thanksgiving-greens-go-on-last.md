The hard part of Thanksgiving is the last 30 minutes. The turkey's resting, every burner is going, the oven's full, and 6 dishes need to land at once.

Microgreens don't belong anywhere in that rush. They go on after it.

## Wednesday night

Our clamshells say wash before using. On Thanksgiving, do it the night before.

Take out what you'll use, wash it, and dry it well. A salad spinner and then a clean towel. Wet greens wilt fast on warm food. Pack the dry greens in a sealed container lined with a paper towel and put it in the fridge. Leave the rest in the clamshell, unwashed, for later in the week.

If you're making the [7-dish version](/blog/seven-greens-one-thanksgiving-table/), split them into small containers by dish now. On Thursday you'll be glad nobody has to sort anything.

## Thursday, last

Nothing green goes in the oven. Not on the casserole, not on the stuffing, not on the sweet potatoes while they finish.

The order is: food comes out, food gets plated or set on the table, then the greens. A small pinch per dish. It takes about 2 minutes and it's the one job in that last half hour a kid can do without anyone worrying.

If the table's a buffet and it sits for a while, put a bowl of greens on ice at the end of the line and let people top their own plates. Same trick as [the tailgate board](/blog/the-tailgate-board/), and it works the same indoors.

## Friday

A leftover turkey sandwich with cranberry is the best meal of the weekend, and [arugula](/greens/arugula/) belongs in it. The pepper cuts through the sweet. Wash a handful out of the clamshell the same way, dry it, and layer it in.

Whatever's left by Saturday: store it dry in the fridge and wash only the portion you're about to eat. Pea, kale, and broccoli hold the longest.

Before Thanksgiving, write "greens" on your Thursday timeline in the slot after the turkey comes out, and put somebody's name next to it.
