It is September.

Back at the start of August we said we would [figure out the fall garden in September](/blog/august-plans-written-in-pencil/), and we said we would probably figure it out later in September than we meant to. That was a joke with a little insurance built into it. It is the eighth, so technically we are still inside our own excuse. The excuse is getting thinner.

Here is the report anyway. Nothing has changed.

No seed order. No varieties picked. No list on the refrigerator, nothing anybody could hold us to that was not equally unheld a month ago. If you came here for a fall planting plan, we do not have one, and we would rather say so now than let it go quiet until October.

What is worth writing down is the reason, because it turned out not to be the reason we assumed.

We had been carrying this around as a weather problem. The heat has not broken, and down here it breaks when it feels like it. But the heat is not really what is holding us up, because nothing stops you from picking varieties in the heat. You just cannot put them in the ground yet.

The actual answer is more boring than weather, and a little more embarrassing.

We do not have anywhere to put it.

The two beds on this property that are finished, proven, and known to grow things are our oldest daughter's, and she is still using them. There is a garden in them right now. Nobody has asked her to wrap it up so the adults can have the good soil in October, and nobody is going to. She runs the food forest mounds as well, which [we have mentioned before](/blog/zones-for-2-17-acres/). What we had not noticed until we went looking for somewhere to plant is how that adds up. Outside the shed, most of what is actually growing on these two acres is hers.

Everything else we might use is still a pile.

Some of them are ready. We keep saying some, and we are going to keep saying it, because [we still have not pulled the caps off and looked](/blog/how-you-know-a-pile-is-finished/). Until somebody does that with a fork and a free afternoon, a count would just be a guess wearing a number.

So the honest state of the fall garden a week into September is that the ground here is either occupied or unopened, and we have not done the one afternoon of work that would tell us which piles are ready to become the other kind.

That part is not the weather. That part is us.

The front three-quarter acre, meanwhile, is getting on fine without any supervision at all. We [went and looked at it a couple of weeks ago](/blog/cover-crop-check-in/) and it is still the least demanding thing on the property.

A journal entry is allowed to say that nothing moved. This one says it, and it names whose fault that is.
