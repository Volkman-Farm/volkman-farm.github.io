It's a fair thing to wonder. [Cilantro](/greens/cilantro/) is on the rack for 18 to 21 days. [Mustard](/greens/mustard/) is ready in 7 to 10. Both cost $3.50 an ounce.

So do the other 5.

## Consistency

The plainest answer is that we wanted one price. You don't need a chart to order, and if you switch from arugula to beet next week, the total doesn't move.

## The difference is smaller than it looks

The second answer is that, for us, the gap in what it takes to grow one variety over another is mostly negligible.

A slow tray holds a spot on the rack longer, and cilantro seed needs cracking before it's sown. Those are real. But every tray still gets the same soil, the same water, the same light, and [the same cut](/blog/where-the-knife-goes/). Next to everything the varieties share, the differences are small.

## What changes the price

Only 2 things: how many ounces you order, and whether you make it a standing weekly order, which takes [10% off](/blog/subscribe-and-save-how-the-10-percent-works/). The variety never does.

Pick the one you'd eat. Price isn't a reason to choose one green over another here, so don't let it be.
