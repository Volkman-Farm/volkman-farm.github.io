The trays get water at pH 6.0. It comes off a whole house filter, the same one the kitchen tap runs on, and then through a Kangen unit that sets the number.

We've mentioned that in passing a few times and [promised it a post of its own](/blog/can-i-grow-microgreens-at-home/). This is that post.

## Why a seedling is the hard case

A tomato plant in the yard has a root system, weeks of growth behind it, and a lot of soil to buffer whatever comes out of the hose. It can shrug off a bad drink.

A microgreen has none of that. It's 8 to 14 days old at harvest. Its whole root system is a few days of white thread sitting in a shallow tray with no soil volume to speak of, and it is drinking constantly because there is nothing else for it to do. Whatever's in the water goes straight into the only part of the plant that can't repair itself in time.

Municipal water is treated to be safe for people, which is the right goal and a different goal. Chlorine and chloramine are in there on purpose. At the concentration that keeps a pipe clean, they're rough on the very fine root hairs a seedling puts out in its first week, and they're rough on the microbial life in the media those roots are trying to work with.

So the filter is not about taste. It's about not sterilizing the thing we're trying to grow.

## The number

6.0 is slightly acidic, and it's where these crops take up nutrients most easily. Off that mark in either direction and the nutrients are still present but harder for the plant to use, which shows up as pale, uneven, slow trays rather than as anything dramatic.

The reason it matters more here than in a garden is the clock again. A tray gets somewhere between 8 and 14 days total. There's no recovering from a rough first week and finishing strong. A tray that stalls on day 3 is just a worse tray on day 12, and you don't get to fix it, you only get to notice.

That's also why we don't guess at it. Water is one of maybe 4 inputs in this whole operation, alongside [seed](/blog/seed-sourcing-who-we-buy-from/), media, and light. With that few variables, being sloppy about one of them is a quarter of the crop.

If you grow at home, this is worth 5 minutes of your attention. Fill a glass from your tap, let it sit overnight, and use that on your trays instead of water straight from the faucet. It's the cheapest version of this and you'll see the difference in the root mat before you see it in the leaf.
