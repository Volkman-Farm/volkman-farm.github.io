Somewhere in the last fifteen years we all agreed to stand at a counter and rub salad with our hands.

There is a real reason for it. Mature kale has had months to build structure, and structure in a leaf means fiber and a waxy surface and cell walls doing their job of holding a plant upright in the wind. Oil, salt, and a few minutes of pressure break some of that down, which is why massaged kale goes darker and softer and stops fighting your teeth. The technique is sound. It is just repair work.

[Micro kale](/greens/kale/) is out of the tray in eight to twelve days. There is no structure to break down yet, because the plant never needed any. Nothing has held it up in weather. It is a stem, two seed leaves, and the first suggestion of a true leaf, and it is tender for the simple reason that it has not had time to be anything else.

So the ritual goes away. No oil, no salt, no ten minutes at the sink. It goes on the plate the way it comes out of the clamshell.

## What it tastes like when nothing is tough

Sweeter than you expect, and green in a clean way rather than a bitter one.

The vegetal note that people either love or fight in mature kale is still in there. It just arrives without the chew, and without the edge that a full grown leaf picks up in heat. Kids who have refused a kale salad their whole lives will eat these, which sounds like a sales line and is mostly just what happens when you take the toughness out of a food and leave the flavor.

It carries the nutrition the plant is famous for. Kale is high in vitamin K, and it brings vitamin A, vitamin C, and lutein along with it. It sits in the same family as broccoli, so it also carries glucosinolates, which is [the compound family we wrote about in July](/blog/broccoli-micros-do-the-heavy-lifting/) and hedged carefully then. Same hedge now.

## The one thing it will not survive

Heat.

Everything that makes micro kale easy also makes it defenseless in a hot pan. There is no structure, remember. Drop it into something simmering and you get a dark limp thread instead of a leaf. Our own variety page says to stir it into pasta in the last thirty seconds of cooking, and that is thirty seconds too many. We are writing the correction up properly tomorrow, because the rule is worth more than one line.

For now: cold applications need nothing, warm applications want the greens landing after the pan is off the burner, and anything you are going to bake wants the kale added when it comes out of the oven.

## Where it earns its keep

Avocado toast, because it holds its shape under a fork instead of sliding off. Grain bowls, where it is the only thing on the plate with any lift to it. On a frittata after it comes out of the oven. Folded into pesto, where it stretches basil and adds a green that does not shout.
