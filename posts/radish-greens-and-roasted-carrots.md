The sweet ends of roasted carrots are the best part. They brown a little more than the thick ends, and they're usually gone before the tray makes it to the table. Save one of those for a taste test.

Put a carrot on a plate and add 2 or 3 radish microgreen shoots. The carrot is sweet, and the radish has a little bite. If you like them together, try another carrot with a spoonful of plain yogurt underneath. It gives the greens somewhere to sit, and the tang works nicely with the browned edges.

Don't add the greens while the carrots are roasting. Set the tray down, move the carrots onto a plate, and then add a pinch where you want it. A few shoots on one end let you taste a bite with radish and a bite without. That's more useful than trying to guess whether the whole plate needs another handful.

If you're already making carrots this week, try this on one before you decide what to serve. Plain roasted carrots will still be good if someone at the table wants to leave them be.
