No.

You can put them in a freezer. They will come back out and they will be dark, limp, and wet, and you will not want to eat them.

The reason is not flavor, it is architecture. A microgreen is mostly water held inside cells, and the crispness you are paying for is water pressure pushing outward against cell walls. Freeze it and that water turns into ice crystals with sharp edges that puncture the walls from the inside. Thaw it and the water runs out, because there is nothing left to hold it in. Full grown spinach survives that treatment because it has enough fiber to still be something afterward. A nine day old seedling does not have any fiber to fall back on. Structure is all it was.

So freezing does not preserve a microgreen. It just delays the moment you find out.

## What to do instead

**Eat them in order.** The tender ones first, the sturdy ones later in the week. [We wrote up shelf life in July](/blog/how-long-do-microgreens-last/) and it still holds: about a week, sometimes ten days, stored dry and closed in the main body of the fridge.

**Freeze them inside something else.** This is the version that actually works, because you are no longer asking the leaf to hold its shape. Blend them into a sauce or a pesto and freeze that in an ice cube tray. Or build [smoothie packs](/blog/the-smoothie-you-wont-taste/) and drop a handful into each bag before it goes in the freezer, since the greens in a smoothie were going to get destroyed by a blade anyway. Nothing is lost by destroying them earlier and colder.

Both of those are your call at home, at your own sink. Our label says wash before using, and that step belongs to the portion you are about to eat.

**Or order less more often.** That is genuinely the better answer for most people, and it is most of why [a standing order](/subscribe/) exists.
