Put a few [pea tendrils](/greens/pea/) across a bowl of noodles before you pick up the fork. Their sweet, grassy flavor and crisp stems give you a different bite from the noodles underneath.

You can use that same green more generously in a sandwich or make it the base of a small salad. You don't need a different variety for every meal.

Our clamshells say wash before using. At home, take out the portion you're about to eat, wash it under running water, and dry it with a clean towel or paper towel. Keep the rest dry in the fridge. The [FDA's produce guide](https://www.fda.gov/food/buy-store-serve-safe-food/selecting-and-serving-produce-safely) covers washing and drying. Use clean hands, dishes, and utensils for each meal.

For a topping, start with a few shoots spread across the bowl. Ladle the noodles and broth first, then add the greens when you're ready to eat. They'll soften as they sit in the hot broth. If you want more crunch, add a few as you go. A topping doesn't need to hide the food underneath it.

For a sandwich filling, use a loose layer across the bread. Hummus, cucumber, and pea tendrils would make a good place to start. The spread helps hold the shoots in place. If longer stems pull out when you bite, give the washed greens a few cuts on a clean board before adding them. Stop piling when you can still close the sandwich comfortably.

For a salad, let pea tendrils fill most of a small bowl. Add cucumber or a few thin apple slices if you want them, then toss with [a little dressing](/blog/a-little-dressing-first/) right before eating. Lemon and olive oil leave room for the pea flavor. You can put this beside the rest of dinner without making a large salad or buying another head of lettuce.

Next time you make a sandwich, try pea tendrils where you'd normally put lettuce. Spread them to the corners so the last bite gets some too.
