There's no chill in the air. It's September in Central Florida and there won't be one for a while.

Soup season starts here anyway, because somebody wants soup, not because the weather asked. And that first pot of the year is where a lot of people put microgreens in exactly the wrong place.

Greens go in the bowl. Never in the pot.

We [made this argument about pasta](/blog/pasta-finishing-greens-at-the-last-second/), and soup is the harder version of it. A pot is wetter, hotter, and stays hot much longer than a pan of noodles. Anything stirred in is gone in about 15 seconds. Not wilted. Gone: color, texture, and most of what you paid for.

So ladle first. Then set the greens on the finished bowl, off to one side instead of dead center, and hand it over. The steam does exactly enough. It softens the leaf, pushes the smell up under whoever's holding the spoon, and leaves the stems with some structure left. They keep wilting down into the soup while it gets eaten, so the last spoonful isn't the same as the first.

## What goes with what

Creamy and mild soups want body. Pea tendrils are the best of these, sweet and substantial enough to read as an ingredient instead of a garnish. Potato soup, squash puree, anything with cream in it.

Brothy and spicy soups want [cilantro](/greens/cilantro/). Tortilla soup, black bean, pho at home, anything getting a squeeze of lime. Micro cilantro is more concentrated than the bunch from the store, so start with less than you think you need.

Heavy long-cooked soups want a little bite to cut them. [Kale](/greens/kale/) does that on a lentil soup or a minestrone, and it holds its shape in steam better than most.

Tomato soup is the one to watch. Acid plus heat is hard on a tender leaf, and the sharp varieties fight the tomato instead of helping it. Go mild there, broccoli, and go light.

Keep the container in the fridge until the bowls are on the table, not out on the counter while the pot simmers. 20 minutes in a warm kitchen and they're half wilted before they ever reach the soup. Cold to hot, at the last second. Our label asks you to wash your portion before using, so do that at your own sink when you're ready to eat.

Next pot you make, hold a pinch back and put it on one bowl and not the other. You'll taste which one you'd rather be eating, and you won't need us to tell you which. [Order for a Friday](/order/) if the drawer's empty.
