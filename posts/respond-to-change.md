One side of the run's fence got pushed down over the weekend of Aug 29. We lost 3 birds, and 2 more went into the brooder.

Nobody plans for that kind of change. We [wrote it up](/blog/we-were-watching-the-sky/) the week it happened.

David Holmgren closes his list of 12 principles with this one: creatively use and respond to change. The proverb he pins to it is "vision is not seeing things as they are but as they will be." He means it as 2 halves. Responding is what you do when change lands on you. Using it is what you do when you see it coming and get there first.

Most of this year has been the first half.

## Responding

The rebuild went up fast. 4 electric poultry nets, 2 on the outside and 2 on the inside, with a 5 ft corridor between them all the way around. [2 paddocks](/blog/two-weeks-and-nothing-has-come-back/) where there used to be 1 run, and an aviary net over the top.

The part I'd point at isn't the nets, though. It's the grass.

My read on the old fence is that it probably wasn't doing its job, and green growth touching a hot wire bleeds the charge off. That's a suspicion. Nobody proved it. But it changed a chore. The fence lines get mowed to about 3 inches now, and that has to keep happening for as long as the fence is up.

Most of responding looks like that. Not a big fix. A new habit nobody had before.

The chick plan got the same treatment, twice. In September it was waiting on 2 hurt birds. They [went back to the flock](/blog/back-with-the-flock/) on Oct 4, and now it's [waiting on our calendar](/blog/picking-a-date-for-chicks/). Nobody wrote a plan that said wait for the calendar. The plan moved when the facts did, and it'll move again.

## Using

The other half is harder, because it means you saw the thing before it got here.

The front field is the closest we've come. Sunn hemp went in this spring. We [chopped it to 18 inches](/blog/chop-and-drop/) about 2 months in, mowed it again on Jul 30, and a 50 lb bag of Hancock's mix went down over the residue on Aug 1. Each of those was timed to what the field was about to do. It got mowed again the week of Sep 28, and right now it's waiting on a decision. That's fine, as long as the mower keeps it from deciding for us.

The food forest mounds are the slow version. They started as mulch piles we parked and left for a year. Nobody called that design at the time. But we knew what a year would do to a pile, and we let it, and the lemon and the mangoes went into what came out.

Seasons are the easiest change to see coming, and we still get caught. Every tray in the shed takes [longer in October](/blog/the-shed-in-october/), and [cilantro](/greens/cilantro/) was already 18 to 21 days in July. We knew fall would stretch the clocks. We wrote the variety pages in July anyway.

## Your list

Walk your place this week with a notebook. Write down 3 changes you can see coming in the next 6 months: a season turning, a tree that's going to shade something, a fence that'll need mowing, a pile that'll be ready.

Pick the one you'd hate to be surprised by, and do one thing about it before it shows up.
