The first post on this blog said that when something died in July, we'd tell you it died in July.

Nothing died in July. 3 birds died at the end of August, and we [told you that week](/blog/we-were-watching-the-sky/).

That first post went up 12 weeks ago today. There's been one every day since, 84 of them before this one, and now there's a written record of this place that didn't exist in June. Some of it has held up. Some of it we've had to correct in print: the chicken tractor that doesn't roam anymore, and the seed that turned out to be weighed for every tray and not scattered by feel. Reading back over all of it, 3 things worked and 1 I'd set up differently from the first day.

The spent trays worked. They worked before the blog started, and they were the one loop the first post could point at as already running. Nothing about it has changed. A harvest ends with a stack of root mats, the mats go in the wheelbarrow, the wheelbarrow goes about 150 feet to the run, and by the next day the flock has scratched a solid slab into something close to dirt. The black soldier fly bin inside the run takes what the birds won't, and the larvae climb out and walk to the chickens on their own. We wrote the full loop up [in July](/blog/the-chickens-run-the-compost/).

It's still a wheelbarrow trip in the heat, every harvest. We've stopped looking for a way around it. What's left of a tray of [the greens](/greens/) still goes to the birds and not the curb.

The front field worked, and it cost us 1 bag. 50 lb of Hancock's spring and summer mix, broadcast on Aug 1 over sunn hemp we'd mowed 2 days earlier. We half expected it to come up blotchy, since throwing seed onto mowed stalks is a rough way to plant. It [came up even](/blog/cover-crop-check-in/), and by late August you had to hunt for bare sand instead of hunting for plants. Nothing else on this property has given back that much for that little.

2 things about the field are still open. In August we promised to tell you which of the 8 things in that bag came up, and that inventory is still owed. And the [chop and drop](/blog/chop-and-drop/) cut still doesn't have a date. We'll walk it and decide.

The paddocks worked, which is a strange thing to put in the win column, because they only exist because something went wrong. After the attack we rebuilt the run bigger and we did it fast: 2 paddocks at 50 by 30 feet, an outer and an inner electric net with a 5 ft corridor between them, a divider down the middle so one side can rest, and the grass along the lines mowed to about 3 inches. It [was the fence](/blog/two-weeks-and-nothing-has-come-back/), in the end. Up the whole time, and not working. Nothing has gotten in since the rebuild.

## The one I'd redo

The air conditioner.

We [mentioned it in July](/blog/keeping-a-grow-shed-cool/) and left it there. The shed started out with a single-hose portable unit, and it didn't cool the room. It ran and ran, and the shed stayed hot and wet.

It took me a while to see why. A single-hose unit cools its own condenser with air it pulls from the room, then blows that air outside through the hose. The room has to replace every bit of that air from somewhere. In a shed, somewhere is every gap around the door and every crack in the walls, and what comes through them is Central Florida outside air (hot and wet, most of the year). So the machine was pulling the room into negative pressure and dragging in the heat and humidity it was supposed to be taking out.

The fix was to stop letting it breathe the room. I built a makeshift plenum around the intake so it pulls from outside, and cut a foam board panel to fit the window with both lines running through it. The room cooled after that. It's the 2-hose setup in there today.

What I'd redo is the order. The unit was never broken. It was doing exactly what it was designed to do, in a room that couldn't afford it, and I spent that stretch troubleshooting a machine when the problem was the air. If I built the shed again, the air conditioner would get its own outside air on day one.

So if you're about to put a portable unit in a shed, a garage, or a back room, count the hoses before you look at anything else on the box. If there's 1, go stand by the door on a hot afternoon and feel for where the room's air is coming back in.
