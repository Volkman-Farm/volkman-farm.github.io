Deviled eggs already have a lot of opinions attached to them. Sweet relish or no relish. Smooth filling or a few lumps. Enough paprika to see from across the table. If you have a version you like, we wouldn't change the filling just to make room for greens.

A few [mustard microgreens](/greens/mustard/) on top are worth trying. Their sharp, horseradish-like flavor is different from the mustard in the jar, and you notice the little leaves against the soft filling. Start with one egg. A small pinch gives you a better idea of the combination than covering the entire platter before you've tasted it.

This is one place where a large handful gets awkward. You want to be able to pick up the egg and eat it without the topping falling into your lap. A couple of short shoots can be enough. If you prefer a milder green, [broccoli](/greens/broccoli/) adds a little texture without the same bite.

You can leave some plain, too. A platter doesn't have to look identical from end to end. Someone who likes the familiar version can have it, and someone else can try the mustard greens without committing everyone to your experiment.

Wash the greens under running water and dry them with a clean towel before adding them. [FDA produce guidance](https://www.fda.gov/food/buy-store-serve-safe-food/selecting-and-serving-produce-safely) advises against soap or produce wash. Keep the finished eggs refrigerated until serving; for a gathering, a small chilled platter is easier to replenish than a large one left on the table. [FDA egg-safety guidance](https://www.fda.gov/food/buy-store-serve-safe-food/what-you-need-know-about-egg-safety) limits unrefrigerated time to 2 hours, or 1 hour above 90°F.

If you're making deviled eggs for Thanksgiving, try the green on one this week. You might decide you want it on half the platter. You might decide the paprika was enough.
