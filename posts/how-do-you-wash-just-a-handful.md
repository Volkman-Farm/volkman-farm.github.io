---
title: How do you wash just a handful?
description: "Wash only the microgreens you're about to eat, dry them gently, and keep the rest cold and dry. A small strainer makes a sandwich-sized portion easier."
date: 2026-11-07T09:00:00-05:00
pillar: Reader questions
---

Our clamshells say wash before using. If you're putting a few greens on a sandwich, take out that much and wash it. The rest can stay dry in the fridge until its turn.

A small strainer makes this easier. Choose one with holes fine enough to hold the stems, and have a clean towel or paper towel beside it before you start.

## At the sink

Wash your hands with soap and water for at least 20 seconds before handling the greens, and again afterward. Use a clean strainer and a clean work surface. Keep both away from raw meat, poultry, seafood, and the utensils used to prepare them.

Take the portion you want out of the clamshell with clean hands or a clean utensil. Close the container and put it back in the fridge. The [FDA recommends 40°F or below](https://www.fda.gov/food/buy-store-serve-safe-food/selecting-and-serving-produce-safely) for perishable produce.

Rinse the portion thoroughly under cool running drinking water. Keep the stream gentle and turn the greens so the water reaches the leaves and stems. The strainer catches the ones that would otherwise head down the drain. Don't leave them soaking in a bowl. [University of Minnesota Extension's washing guide](https://extension.umn.edu/food/preparing/cooking-at-home/washing-fresh-fruits-and-vegetables) covers delicate produce and recommends a final rinse under running water.

Let the water drain, then lay the greens on the clean towel and pat them gently. No squeezing or wringing. Once they're dry enough for the meal, put them on the food. A wet handful can leave a sandwich soggy and dilute the [dressing you just tasted](https://volkman.farm/blog/a-little-dressing-first/).

## What goes in the water

Plain water is enough for this job. Keep soap, detergent, bleach, and household disinfectants off the greens. The [FDA does not recommend commercial produce washes](https://www.fda.gov/food/buy-store-serve-safe-food/selecting-and-serving-produce-safely), either. Vinegar and lemon juice belong in the dressing; [FoodSafety.gov says they have not been shown to remove germs effectively](https://www.foodsafety.gov/blog/safe-ways-handle-and-clean-produce).

Washing can reduce bacteria, but it won't remove all of them. It also doesn't make rotten greens usable. Discard those.

You may have a grocery-store package marked pre-washed or ready-to-eat. The FDA says those products can be used without another wash. Follow the package in front of you. Ours says wash before using.

For the next sandwich, set the strainer beside the plate. Wash what you're eating, dry it gently, and keep the remaining greens cold for the next meal.
