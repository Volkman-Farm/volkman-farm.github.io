Most of what we grow is green. [Arugula](/greens/arugula/), [broccoli](/greens/broccoli/), [mustard](/greens/mustard/), and [cilantro](/greens/cilantro/) all land on a plate as small green leaves, and that's what you buy them for: flavor first.

A few of ours do something a little different, and it's worth knowing which so you put them where they'll show.

## Beet: the pink

[Beet](/greens/beet/) micros grow on bright pink stems with a green leaf on top. It's the only one of ours with real color in it.

The pink disappears into anything red or brown. Put it where there's nothing else that color: on white fish, goat cheese, a pale soup, scrambled eggs, a bowl of rice. On a dark plate it's even louder.

## Pea tendrils: the curl

[Pea tendrils](/greens/pea/) curl on their own, and we grow them a little taller than the others so the curl has room. That makes them the one green that adds height.

Stand a few up instead of laying them flat. On top of a burger, a stack of pancakes, a mound of rice, a single tendril pointing up reads from across the table.

## Kale: the ruffle

We grow a curly variety of [kale](/greens/kale/). The leaves come out ruffled at the edges, so a pile of them has more texture than a pile of flat leaves. It's a small thing. It's the difference between a garnish that looks scattered and one that looks put there.

## Cilantro, a little

Cilantro micros have thinner stems than the herb, so they sit light. A pinch on top of a taco or a bowl of pho reads delicate where chopped cilantro reads like confetti.

Next time you plate dinner, pick one green for its color or shape, not only its flavor, and put it last, on top, where it'll be the first thing anybody sees.
