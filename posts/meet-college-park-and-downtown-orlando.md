This is the fourth of these neighborhood posts, and it is the odd one, because the two places in it are not on the same route and do not have anything to do with each other operationally.

[College Park](/delivery/college-park/) is the last stop on the north Orlando run, Monday and Wednesday evenings, after [Casselberry, Maitland, and Winter Park](/blog/meet-casselberry-maitland-winter-park/). [Downtown](/delivery/downtown-orlando/) is not on that run at all. Downtown gets a route of its own on Sunday afternoon, for a reason that has nothing to do with efficiency.

They are together in one post because they are the two Orlando neighborhoods we go to, and because they have the same thing underneath them.

## College Park was a grove

Not a metaphor. An actual citrus grove.

The first known house in what is now College Park was built by a citrus grower, and it went up in the middle of an eighty acre grove. That is what the land was for. Orlando in that era was a citrus town in the plainest sense, and the ground north and west of downtown was rows of trees belonging to people who made a living off them.

Then the twenties happened. Florida got a land boom, Orlando pushed its northern boundary out, and developers started laying subdivisions across the groves. Somebody had the idea of naming the streets after colleges, which is why you can drive Princeton and Harvard and Yale in a neighborhood where no college has ever stood. The name of the whole place comes from the street signs. Dubsdread opened in 1924 as part of the same wave and is still a public course. Jack Kerouac lived in a small house on Clouser Avenue in the late fifties and wrote there, which the neighborhood has never once let anybody forget, and fairly so.

What you have today is one of the most walkable neighborhoods in Orlando, a corridor of shops along Edgewater, and a street grid of bungalows sitting on top of what was a working agricultural property inside of one lifetime.

The evening route ends there twice a week, on a street grid that was rows of fruit trees inside of one lifetime. There is no lesson in that, but we think about it every time we look at the map.

## Downtown is a Sunday, and it is a church schedule

We have said this three times now so we will say it once and link the long version: [the routes are a family calendar, not a sales territory](/blog/do-you-deliver-to-my-street/). Monday and Wednesday evenings are taekwondo and rock climbing. Friday morning is home. And Sunday afternoon exists because our church is in Parramore, so we are already downtown.

Parramore is worth more than a passing mention if we are going to name it as the reason we drive down there every week. It was established in the 1880s and it became the historical hub of Orlando's African American community, with its own businesses and institutions and a long stretch of decades where it was the center of Black life in this city. When the interstate came through in the 1960s it cut the neighborhood off from the downtown it sits next to, and a lot of what was there did not survive that. Some of it is still standing. Some of it is coming back. We are there on a Sunday morning, which is not the same as being from there, and we are not going to write about it as though it were.

The rest of downtown is a different animal from every other stop we have. High rises around Lake Eola, Thornton Park, Mills 50, North Quarter, condo buildings where the porch is a hallway and the delivery instructions are a floor number. Everything about a downtown drop is tighter than a suburban one.

## What the two runs are actually designed around

Different constraints, different answers.

**The north Orlando run is designed around dinner.** We cut in the afternoon and drop by dinner, which was the entire point of putting that route in the evening, and it means College Park at the end of the run still gets greens that were in a tray that same day. Being last on a route costs something, and [we have been straight about what](/blog/meet-winter-springs-and-oviedo/). Here the cost is small, because Casselberry to Maitland to Winter Park to College Park is a short tight line down a corridor we would be driving anyway. Last on this route is a matter of minutes, not hours.

**The downtown run is designed around the week ahead.** Sunday is not a dinner delivery, it is a setup delivery. Greens cut Sunday morning land Sunday afternoon and then sit in a fridge waiting for a Tuesday that has not happened yet. That changes what we would tell you to order. On a Friday route the tender things are fine, because Friday night is right there. On a Sunday drop the sturdier varieties earn their place, [kale](/greens/kale/) and broccoli among them, and the delicate ones want to be eaten early in the week rather than saved.

The porch part is the same everywhere and the [clock still starts when the clamshell lands](/blog/when-the-bag-lands-on-your-porch/). Downtown adds one wrinkle, which is that a condo lobby or a shaded breezeway is often better than an actual doorstep, and if that is your building, tell us and we will use it every time.
