There are 2 birds in the brooder, and most of next year is waiting on them.

They're the 2 that were hurt at the end of August. They went in there [the week it happened](/blog/we-were-watching-the-sky/) because it was the smallest, warmest space we had, and [2 weeks later](/blog/two-weeks-and-nothing-has-come-back/) they were improving and still in it. They're still recovering. Still in the brooder.

A brooder is for chicks. So until those 2 come out, there's nowhere to put new ones, and new chicks are the first thing on next year's list. We'll order them as soon as the brooder's empty. Not before.

After the chicks, I need to build a second chicken tractor. We'll write that one up once it exists.

The paddocks we rebuilt after the attack have something we never mentioned: they're covered. There's an aviary net over the top. So the flock has a net against hawks, and at ground level 2 lines of electric netting with a 5 ft corridor between them. The divider down the middle still has the second job we gave it in the rebuild post (keeping young birds apart from the flock but in sight of it), and it'll be helpful when we introduce the chicks.

The spent trays from [the greens](/greens/) keep going out to the run, same as now, just to more birds.

So the list runs brooder, then chicks, then tractor. Nothing on it can jump the one ahead of it, and the thing at the top isn't a project at all. It's 2 birds getting better on their own schedule.

## What's not on it

Water. We've written that a well and a cistern are planned, and they still are. Neither one is happening next year. [The rain post](/blog/the-rain-we-get-and-the-rain-we-lose/) said nothing on this property is built to hold water, and that'll stay true through next year. The low spots will keep pooling for 2 or 3 days after a hard storm, and we'll keep watching where.

Trees are halfway on it. We will plant trees next year. We haven't picked any. But the where is mostly settled already, because the ground settled it. The contours put trees on the higher ground and the water-tolerant stuff in the lows, and [the shade post](/blog/shade-is-an-asset-not-a-problem/) decided the front field stays open for the cover crop. So we know where the holes go before we know what goes in them. Most people shop for the tree first and then walk around the yard holding it. We'd like to do it the other way.

Our oldest daughter's beds aren't on the list either. They don't need a plan from us. She's out there keeping them going now, and next year they'll be whatever she makes of them.

Write down your own list for next year. Next to every line, write what has to happen before you can start it. If the answer is "nothing," that one's ready. If it's small and dull, a box that has to empty out, move it to the top, because everything underneath is waiting on it whether it looks like a project or not.
