Mostly, no.

A microgreen is a seedling, and a seedling keeps its growing point right up top, between the first leaves. Cut just above the soil and you've taken that point with you. What's left is a stub of stem with nowhere to grow from. It'll sit there green for a day or 2 and then quit.

So a tray gets 1 harvest. Ours get cut once, [straight across with a clean blade](/blog/harvest-morning-timeline/), and what's left, soil, roots, and stubs, goes out to the chickens.

## The exception is pea

[Pea](/greens/pea/) grows differently. Along the stem there are joints where a leaf comes off, and each one can push a new shoot. Cut a pea above the lowest of those joints instead of at the soil and it'll often come back.

The second cut is smaller, slower, and tougher. Growers who try it tend to report somewhere around a third to half of the first cut, and the stems that come back aren't as tender as the ones that grew straight from seed.

If you're [growing at home](/blog/can-i-grow-microgreens-at-home/), try it once on a tray of pea. Cut half the tray at the soil and the other half above the first joint, and give it 2 weeks. Then taste a second-cut shoot next to a fresh one and decide for yourself whether the tray was worth keeping.
