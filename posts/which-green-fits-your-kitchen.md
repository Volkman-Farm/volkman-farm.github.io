7 varieties are cuttable right now. 3 aren't.

The 3 that aren't are radish, kohlrabi, and Swiss chard, and we're leading with that because a roundup that points you at greens you can't order is a catalog, not an answer. When they're back we'll say so. Until then, ignore anybody who tells you a farm always has everything.

So, the other 7, sorted the way you'd decide.

## If somebody at your table is suspicious of greens

**Broccoli.** Mild, faintly nutty, and most people can't identify it blind. It's the one we hand to a skeptic, and it's why it does [the heavy lifting](/blog/broccoli-micros-do-the-heavy-lifting/) in this house. 8 to 10 days.

**Kale**, for the same reason and a different flavor. Sweeter than the mature leaf with none of the toughness, so it skips the entire [argument about massaging](/blog/kale-you-dont-have-to-massage/) that kale salads usually start. 8 to 12 days.

Neither of these changes a dish. They add to one.

## If you want the plate to taste different

**Mustard** is the sharp one. Horseradish heat that builds and then backs off, and [the heat peaks at harvest](/blog/mustard-brings-the-heat/), so what you get on Friday is the strongest it will ever be. Use a smaller hand than you think. 7 to 10 days.

**Arugula** is the same neighborhood, less aggressive. Pepper without the bitterness the grown plant develops, plus a cut-grass note underneath. If you've had mature arugula go harsh on you in July, [this is not that](/blog/arugula-before-it-gets-bitter/). About 8 days.

**Cilantro** is the specialist, and the one worth ordering on purpose. It is unmistakably cilantro, brighter and more concentrated than the bunch at the store, and it converts people who thought they hated the herb. It's also the slowest thing we grow at 18 to 21 days, which is why it can never ride along in a mixed tray.

## If you want it to look like somebody tried

**Pea tendrils.** Sweet, grassy, exactly like cracking a snap pea open, and the tendrils curl on their own. They read as an ingredient rather than a garnish, which makes them the best of the lot on soup and the [easiest first order](/blog/pea-tendrils-the-gateway-green/) for someone who has never bought microgreens. 10 to 14 days.

**Beet.** Earthy and slightly sweet with a crisp stem and deep color. Slow at about 14 days because the seed is stubborn about waking up, and worth the wait when the plate is otherwise beige.

## The short version by meal

Eggs and sandwiches, broccoli or kale. Tacos and anything with lime, cilantro. Steak, roast vegetables, or a plate that needs a fight, mustard or arugula. Soup and salad where the green is the point, pea. A dish that needs color, beet.

Everything is $3.50 an ounce, same for all of them, so the choice is entirely about your kitchen and not about your budget.

Pick the one that matches what you cooked last night, not the one that sounds most interesting. [Order 2 ounces of it](/order/) and see whether it changes the plate. If it does, you've found yours, and if it doesn't, you're out one week and you know something.
