The harvest knife is very sharp, so I'm the one who holds it.

Every other job in the shed gets shared. That one doesn't. The jobs go to whoever can do them safely and whoever is home, and a small operation only works if everybody knows which ones are theirs.

There are 4 jobs between a bag of seed and a porch.

## Seeding and tray prep

Me and our oldest daughter.

A clean tray gets about 6 cups of Fox Farm Happy Frog, leveled flat. Then the seed, [weighed for every tray](/blog/every-tray-gets-weighed/), a different amount per variety and spread in one even layer. A first drink of filtered water, then the tray goes into [the blackout phase](/blog/the-blackout-phase/) and nobody touches it for a few days.

It's the job with the most steps and the least drama. Nothing about it can hurt you. Everything about it can go wrong quietly, and you won't find out for a week, which is why the scale matters more than speed does.

Our oldest daughter also manages the food forest out back.

## Harvest

Me, with the knife.

First light, a clean blade straight across just above the soil line, into the clamshell and onto the scale. [Nothing touches the crop after the blade](/blog/harvest-morning-timeline/). A tray of [pea tendrils](/greens/pea/) goes fast. A tray of [cilantro](/greens/cilantro/) goes fast and then stays on you for the rest of the day, which I don't mind.

A cut tray is the only point in the process where a mistake costs a finger instead of a tray, so it doesn't rotate.

## Packing

The kids.

Clamshells closed and into the insulated cooler bag. It's quick work and it's safe work, and it's the job that turns a harvest into something that can leave the property.

## Driving

Me or my wife.

The cooler bag goes into the back of [the family car](/blog/the-car-is-the-warehouse/) and whoever is driving takes it. The route days were set by [the calendar we already had](/blog/why-we-stay-small-on-purpose/), so the driver is usually whoever was going that direction anyway.

If you're growing [at home](/blog/can-i-grow-microgreens-at-home/) with kids in the house, give them the packing and the watering and keep the blade for yourself. Then write down who does what, even on a sticky note on the shed door. You'll find out which job nobody has been doing.
