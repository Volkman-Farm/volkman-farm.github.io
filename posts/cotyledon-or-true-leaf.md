The first 2 leaves on a seedling aren't leaves. They're the seed, opened up.

Cotyledons are packed inside the seed before anything is planted, and they come up already built, carrying the food the plant lives on until it can feed itself. They're usually rounded, usually plain, and they look about the same across wildly different plants. Broccoli and kale and arugula all come up looking like the same small green comma.

Then the true leaves arrive between them, and those look like the plant. Serrated on arugula. Ruffled on kale. That's the moment the seedling stops running on stored food and starts running on light.

Most of what we sell is cut at the cotyledon stage or right at the changeover. That's not a rule we inherited, it's where the eating is best.

## What changes at the turn

Flavor concentrates. A true leaf is the plant committing to being what it's going to be, and for the sharp varieties that means more of it. Mustard and arugula both get harder to use as the true leaves come in, which is the same reason [mature arugula goes harsh in summer](/blog/arugula-before-it-gets-bitter/) and micro arugula doesn't.

Texture goes the wrong direction. Cotyledons are tender the whole way through. True leaves start building the structure a full-size plant needs, which is fine on a plate you're going to chew through and wrong on something you wanted to melt into an egg.

And the stem stretches. Waiting for true leaves means a taller, leggier tray, and past a certain point the tray starts leaning on itself.

## It's different per variety

[Broccoli](/blog/broccoli-micros-do-the-heavy-lifting/) we cut right as the first true leaves show, at 8 to 10 days, which is its peak.

[Pea](/blog/pea-tendrils-the-gateway-green/) is the exception that proves it. We grow it deliberately taller, 10 to 14 days, because the tendril is the product and a tendril needs length to curl. Cutting pea at the cotyledon stage would be throwing away the reason to buy it.

[Cilantro](/greens/cilantro/) takes 18 to 21 days and is the slowest thing on the rack, so its whole schedule sits outside everybody else's. [Beet](/greens/beet/) is around 14 and stubborn about germinating in the first place.

[Arugula](/greens/arugula/) is out in about 8, [mustard](/blog/mustard-brings-the-heat/) in 7 to 10, [kale](/greens/kale/) in 8 to 12.

That spread is why [a mixed tray has to share one cut date](/blog/whats-in-the-salad-mix/) and why the slow varieties can never be in one.

Next time you get a clamshell, look at a single shoot before you eat it. Find the 2 plain rounded leaves at the bottom and then look for the small different-shaped one pushing up between them. Whether it's there tells you exactly what day that tray was on when it was cut.
