We get our chicks from [Lazzzy D Farm](https://lazzzydfarm.com/). We asked about the next batch, and the earliest they could have hatchlings for us is Dec 5.

Dec 5 is a fine day to be a chick. It's a bad day for us to start.

Last week I wrote that [the date has to clear the holidays](/blog/picking-a-date-for-chicks/), because chicks in a brooder need somebody home every day until they're feathered out. Count forward from Dec 5 and those weeks land right on top of our holiday schedule. It'd be a stretch to cover them.

So we're waiting. New chicks come in February.

The chick plan has moved a few times since September. It waited on 2 hurt birds, then on our calendar, and now it's got a couple of months tacked on. The brooder will still be there in February.

The second chicken tractor still comes after the chicks, same as before.

Until then the flock we've got keeps its job. Spent trays out of the shed go to the run, so whatever's left of a cut tray of [kale](/greens/kale/) or [cilantro](/greens/cilantro/), roots and soil and all, ends up scratched apart by the hens.

If you're thinking about chicks this winter, get the calendar out before you ask about a hatch date. Count forward from the day they'd come home to the day they'd be feathered out, and read what's already written on those weeks.
