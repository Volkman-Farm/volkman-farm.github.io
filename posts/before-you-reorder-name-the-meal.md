The clamshell is empty, but the useful detail is what emptied it. Sandwiches? A bowl of noodles? A little on everyone's eggs?

Before ordering again, write down the green and the meal where you liked it most. A phone note is enough: “Pea, lunch sandwiches, liked the crunch.” You don't need a rating scale or a food diary.

The [first choice](/blog/which-green-fits-your-kitchen/) can be a guess. The next one can use what happened in your kitchen.

Separate flavor from quantity. If you liked a green but used less than expected, a different variety may not be the answer. Look at the meals you planned and the meals you ate. A week with several dinners out tells you less about your usual needs than a week at home.

If you used the greens often but wanted a different flavor, keep the quantity similar while trying another variety. Broccoli is a mild choice. Mustard brings a sharper bite. Switching both the variety and the amount at once makes it harder to tell which change helped.

You can also keep a favorite and try a second green for a specific dish. Cilantro has a clear place on tacos; it doesn't have to replace the pea tendrils you liked in a sandwich. Check what's available before planning around a new variety.

Leftovers deserve a note too, without turning lunch into an assignment. Was the flavor too strong? Did you forget the container was there? Did the dinner you bought it for get canceled? Those are different problems. Buying more won't fix any of them.

When you talk with us about your next order, concrete details help. “We liked the broccoli on eggs, but didn't use as much as we expected” gives us something to work with. You don't have to know the right number of ounces before starting that conversation.

Pick 1 meal you're likely to make this week. Write the green beside it before you reorder.
