We grow microgreens, but we don't think your salad needs to be made entirely of them. Lettuce has plenty going for it. A big piece of romaine has a crunch you won't get from a small shoot, and sometimes that's what you want on your fork.

Microgreens are a different ingredient. A little [arugula](/greens/arugula/) adds a peppery flavor. [Pea tendrils](/greens/pea/) are sweeter, with stems and curls that feel different among the lettuce. They can be part of the salad without becoming most of it.

That leaves a lot of room for the food you already like. There's no reason to give up the cucumber, change the dressing or track down a recipe. And if you try a green and don't like it in that combination, it might belong on a sandwich instead. You haven't wasted the rest of the bowl finding that out.

We'd much rather someone find a small use for our greens that they enjoy than buy a large amount because they think they're supposed to. A few shoots with lunch counts. So does putting them in a dish beside the salad for whoever wants some.

The preparation is small too. Our label says to wash before eating: running water, followed by drying with a clean towel. [FDA guidance](https://www.fda.gov/food/buy-store-serve-safe-food/selecting-and-serving-produce-safely) says not to use soap or produce wash. Follow the label on the lettuce as well; a ready-to-eat salad mix doesn't need the same preparation as a whole head.

The lettuce can stay on the shopping list. There's room for both.
