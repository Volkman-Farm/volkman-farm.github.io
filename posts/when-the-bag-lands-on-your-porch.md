Bring it inside. That is the whole answer and everything after this is detail.

One small correction to the question first: no bag lands on your porch. What is sitting there is the clamshell by itself. The insulated cooler bag is ours and it leaves with us, because it is doing its job in the back of the car between stops, not on your step. Once the clamshell is down, nothing is keeping it cold except your porch, and in August your porch is not on your side.

That is why the minutes after a drop are the only part of this you have to care about. Everything else is already handled. The greens were cut at sunrise, they rode cold, and they landed at your door in the same shape they left the shed in.

We [text before we drop](/blog/the-text-before-the-drop/) partly for this reason. The text is not a courtesy. It is us telling you the clock started.

## If you are not going to be home

Tell us and we will work with it.

There is usually a shaded corner, a side door, a spot behind a column that stays out of the sun all morning, and if you point us at it we will use it every week without being asked again. Better still, leave a cooler out with an ice pack in it. That turns a four hour wait into a non-event, and it is the version we would ask for if you offered.

What we would rather you not do is let a clamshell sit in direct sun on a concrete step until dinner. It will still be greens. They will just have spent their best hours somewhere hot, and you paid for those hours.

## Then it is a fridge question

Once it is inside, this stops being about us.

Put it in the main body of the fridge or the crisper, not the door. Leave it closed and leave it dry. Do not wash the whole clamshell and put it back, because wet leaves in a closed container go slimy fast. Our label says wash before using, and that means the handful you are about to eat, at the sink, right before it goes on the plate. We wrote up [the whole shelf life question](/blog/how-long-do-microgreens-last/) in July and it still stands: about a week, sometimes ten days, and the tender varieties want to be eaten first.

That is genuinely it. Bring it in, keep it cold, keep it dry, eat the delicate ones early.
