You won't find any in ours. [The knife goes](/blog/where-the-knife-goes/) as close to the soil as it can without taking roots or dirt, and every variety from [broccoli](/greens/broccoli/) to cilantro gets the same cut. What comes off the blade is what goes in the clamshell.

So this one's mostly for people growing their own.

The roots aren't poisonous. They're the same plant. Sprouts get eaten roots and all, and that's most of the [difference between a sprout and a microgreen](/blog/are-microgreens-just-sprouts/): a sprout is the whole seedling out of a jar of water, a microgreen gets cut above the soil.

The trouble with microgreen roots is where they grew. Ours grow in Fox Farm Happy Frog, about 6 cups to a tray, and by harvest the roots have wrapped through all of it. A root mat holds on to soil. Nobody's picking it apart grain by grain at the sink, and what you'd get for the effort is stringy and tastes like dirt.

The white fuzz you'll see on them before harvest is usually [root hairs, not mold](/blog/root-hairs-or-mold/). It doesn't change the answer.

If you grow at home, cut above the soil line and give the rest of the tray to something that wants it. Ours goes to the chickens. A compost pile takes it just as well.
