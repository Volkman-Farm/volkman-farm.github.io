Nothing goes in the ground this month. That is not a complaint, it is just August in Central Florida. Anything tender you set out now spends its short life being cooked, and anything that does survive is going to be a weaker plant than the same seed would give you in six weeks. So August is the month you sit inside in the afternoon and make plans for fall.

We make a lot of them. We also write them in pencil, and that is the part worth explaining.

Up north you can circle a date. Last frost, first frost, a calendar that mostly behaves. Down here the fall planting window does not open on a date. It opens when the heat finally breaks, and the heat breaks when it feels like it. Some years that is late September. Some years you are still waiting well into October and starting to take it personally. Hurricane season runs through November on top of that. A plan with firm dates on it is not a plan here. It is a wish with a calendar stapled to it.

So we plan in shapes instead. Where beds go, roughly how many, what will be ready by then. What actually goes in them gets decided when the weather says we can.

Which brings us to the honest part. We do not have a list yet. No seed order, no varieties picked, nothing written down that anybody could hold us to. We know the shape of this fall and not the contents of it, and in early August that is genuinely where we are. We will figure it out in September, probably later in September than we mean to.

## One thing did get written in ink

Saturday the front three-quarter acre got its next seed mix, Hancock's Spring and Summer food plot blend, broadcast over a field we had mowed two days before. The part we like about it is that sunn hemp is in the blend, so this is less a replacement for the sunn hemp that has been out there all season than a widening of it. More legumes going in alongside, plus a few fast growers whose whole job is to come up first while the slower things get established. That one was not tentative. It went in.

We said back when we tried to [explain permaculture without the jargon](/blog/permaculture-without-the-jargon/) that a few things were still ahead of us and would show up here as each one became real. This is one of them becoming real, on a Saturday, with no ceremony at all. We are writing up the whole cover crop question properly tomorrow, so we will leave the details there rather than spend them twice.

The other thing that is close to certain: some of the mulch piles are ready. A pile that sits long enough stops being a pile and turns into a bed, and some of ours have put in the time. Our oldest daughter's second bed [started life as one of those](/blog/the-chickens-run-the-compost/), and she did not build it so much as open it up and find soil waiting. We will know which ones are ready when we pull the top off and look, which is not something you can schedule in August either.

That is the whole method, really, and it is the same lesson [the sand handed us in week one](/blog/what-florida-sand-taught-us/). You park something on a spot and come back later. The coming back later is the plan.

## The oldest pencil marks on the page

A well and a rainwater cistern have been on our list since before we moved a wheelbarrow. Both are still plans. Neither one is dug, drilled, ordered, or scheduled, and we are not going to write about them like they exist, because they do not. They are just the two lines on the page that have been erased and rewritten the most times.

We will get to them. That sentence has been true for a while now, which is exactly why it belongs in a post about pencil.

Here is what we have made our peace with. A plan you can erase is not a weak plan, it is the only kind that survives a Florida fall. The people who get hurt here are the ones who commit in August to a date in October, then plant on that date because they said they would, and lose the bed to a week of heat nobody promised would be over. We would rather show up in September with a soft plan and good ground.

The greens, meanwhile, do not care what month it is. They are on a shelf in [a room we keep cool](/blog/keeping-a-grow-shed-cool/), on a schedule the weather is not invited to. [A standing order](/subscribe/) runs on that clock instead of this one.
