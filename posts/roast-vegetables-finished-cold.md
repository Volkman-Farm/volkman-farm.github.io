A sheet pan of roast vegetables comes out of the oven brown, soft, and sweet. All of that is good. What it doesn't have is anything bright, cold, or crisp.

A handful of microgreens fixes that. Not in the oven. On top, after.

Wash the portion you're using the way the clamshell label says, and dry it well. Wet greens on a hot pan steam and go limp before they ever reach the table.

## Sweet potatoes and squash

Sweet potato, butternut, acorn squash. All of these get sweeter in the oven, and sweet wants pepper.

[Arugula](/greens/arugula/) is the one. A loose pile on top of a pan of roasted sweet potato wedges, a squeeze of lime, a little flaky salt. The heat of the vegetables softens the arugula at the bottom of the pile and the top stays crisp.

## Brussels sprouts and broccoli

Roasted brassicas get nutty and a little bitter at the charred edges. [Mustard](/greens/mustard/) on top is more brassica, and it works because the heat is sharper than the bitterness. A small pinch per serving. It builds.

If mustard's too much for your table, [broccoli](/greens/broccoli/) micros on roasted broccoli is a fun one. Same plant, 2 ends of its life, on one fork.

## Roasted roots

Carrots, beets, parsnips, potatoes. [Beet](/greens/beet/) micros on roasted beets look like you planned it, and the pink stems show up against orange carrots too. [Cilantro](/greens/cilantro/) is good on roasted carrots with cumin.

## How much

About a loose handful per sheet pan, scattered right before it goes to the table. Don't toss it in. Tossed with hot vegetables, the greens wilt into the oil and disappear.

Next sheet pan you make, set aside a corner with no greens on it. Taste that corner against the rest, and decide for yourself whether the cold top earns its spot.
