[Broccoli](/greens/broccoli/) and [kale](/greens/kale/) are the same plant.

Not cousins. Same species, *Brassica oleracea*, bred over centuries in different directions: one for the flower head, one for the leaf. At 10 days old, before either has had the chance to become what it was bred for, they're closer still. Put a pinch of each on your tongue and you'll taste how little separates them.

Plant families are useful that way. The 7 greens we grow right now come from only 4 of them, and once you know which is which, you can predict a flavor you haven't tried yet.

## The mustard family: 4 of 7

Broccoli, kale, [mustard](/greens/mustard/), and [arugula](/greens/arugula/). More than half the shelf.

What they share is a set of sulfur compounds called glucosinolates. When you chew the leaf, they break down into the bite the family is known for. How much bite depends on the plant. Mustard turns it all the way up. Arugula is peppery. Broccoli and kale keep it low enough that most people call them mild.

So if you like one and want to try another, move along that line. Like kale? Broccoli is next door. Like arugula and want more? Mustard.

## The other 3, one each

**[Beet](/greens/beet/)** is in the amaranth family, along with spinach, chard, and amaranth itself. Earthy and a little sweet, with the pink stems. When we [tried an amaranth tray](/blog/did-the-amaranth-work/) for color in August, it was a relative stepping in.

**[Cilantro](/greens/cilantro/)** is in the carrot family, with parsley and dill. Nothing else on our shelf tastes like it, and nothing substitutes for it.

**[Pea](/greens/pea/)** is a legume, related to beans and clover. Sweet and grassy, like a snap pea.

## Swapping when you're out

Within a family, a swap usually works. Out of broccoli for eggs? Kale. Out of arugula for a pizza? Mustard, with a lighter hand.

Across families, it usually doesn't. Pea won't stand in for cilantro on a taco, and beet won't stand in for mustard on roast beef. If the green you want isn't around, look for its family first.

Next time you order 2 kinds, pick them from different families. You'll get more range out of the same 2 clamshells.
