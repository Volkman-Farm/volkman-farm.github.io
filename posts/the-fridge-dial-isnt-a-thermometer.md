A fridge dial numbered 1 through 5 doesn't tell you the temperature beside your greens. Neither does the cold air on your hand when you open the door.

We say to keep microgreens refrigerated. The useful number to go with that advice is **40°F or below**. The [FDA recommends an appliance thermometer](https://www.fda.gov/food/buy-store-serve-safe-food/refrigerator-thermometers-cold-facts-about-food-safety) so you can check what the fridge is doing, then adjust its setting if needed.

Use a thermometer made for the refrigerator and follow its placement instructions. Give it time to settle before reading it. If you change the fridge setting, check again after the temperature has stabilized. Keep checking regularly, instead of assuming the setting you chose months ago is still doing the job.

Leave enough room around the clamshell for cold air to circulate. A shelf packed tight with groceries makes that harder. Keep the lid closed, and keep the greens separate from raw meat, poultry, and seafood, including anything that could drip onto the container.

The greens stay dry in storage. Our label says wash before using, so at home, [wash the portion you're about to eat](/blog/how-do-you-wash-just-a-handful/) under running water and dry it with a clean towel or paper towel. Leave the rest in the fridge. The [FDA's produce guide](https://www.fda.gov/food/buy-store-serve-safe-food/selecting-and-serving-produce-safely) covers washing and keeping produce separate from raw animal foods.

We also need to correct something in our July [storage post](/blog/how-long-do-microgreens-last/). It went too far when it said greens that stand up and smell green are fine. Appearance and smell can warn you about spoilage. They can't prove food is safe. The [FDA makes that distinction](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/are-you-storing-food-safely): harmful bacteria can be present without an unusual smell, taste, or appearance.

If the fridge has stopped cooling and you don't know how long perishable food has been warm, don't taste it to decide whether to keep it. Follow the FDA's advice and throw it out. Turning the fridge colder afterward doesn't erase the time the food spent warm.

Next time you put the clamshell away, read the thermometer too.
