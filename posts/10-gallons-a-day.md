The old air conditioner filled a 5 gallon bucket twice a day.

Twice a day, every day, we carried it out of the shed and dumped it.

On Oct 9 that unit came out and a window unit went in. 2 weeks later, it seems to have fixed the problem.

## Where the water came from

An air conditioner pulls water out of the air while it cools it. Warm air crosses a cold coil, the moisture condenses on it, and it has to go somewhere. In our portable floor unit, somewhere was a bucket on the shed floor.

A room full of [trays](/greens/) makes a lot of humidity. We water every day and the plants [breathe it right back out](/blog/keeping-a-grow-shed-cool/). 10 gallons a day is what that looked like when you had to carry it.

A window unit sits half outside. The cold coil faces the room, the hot side faces the yard, and the water it pulls out of the shed's air drains out the back, outdoors. Nothing to carry.

## A correction

On Oct 2 we wrote that the [2-hose setup](/blog/the-shed-in-october/) was the same one that had been running since the fix. A week later it was gone. That post was true the day it ran.

The portable had a history. It started as a single-hose unit that couldn't cool the room, and the plenum and foam board panel that got it working are in our [12-week look back](/blog/12-weeks-in-taking-stock/). It cooled after that. It never stopped filling the bucket.

The window unit hasn't seen a Florida July yet. That's the real test, and we'll say how it went.

If you're growing indoors with a portable unit, keep track of what your bucket collects for a week. It's the plainest number you'll get for how much water is in that room's air, and it's worth knowing before you buy the next machine.
