The paper bag is on the counter. Dinner is cooked, somebody else washed the pans, and you don't need a recipe.

Microgreens can fit that meal too. You don't have to cook from scratch to use them, and buying greens doesn't commit you to a week of elaborate dinners.

Start with what's already in the box. A burger with a rich sauce might suit a little [arugula](/greens/arugula/). A taco with lime might suit [cilantro](/greens/cilantro/). If you want crunch without a strong peppery flavor, [pea tendrils](/greens/pea/) are another option. These are suggestions to taste, not combinations you have to like.

Keep the first try small. Put a few shoots on one bite before adding them to the rest. A takeout meal may already have plenty of sauce, pickles or fresh herbs. More flavor isn't always what it needs. Sometimes a different texture is enough; sometimes the meal is good as it came.

Wash the portion you're about to eat under running water, then dry it with a clean towel. That's your preparation step, as our label says. The [FDA's produce guidance](https://www.fda.gov/food/buy-store-serve-safe-food/selecting-and-serving-produce-safely) recommends running water, not soap or produce wash. Our [small-portion guide](/blog/how-do-you-wash-just-a-handful/) walks through it without soaking the rest of the container.

Add the greens to your serving when you're ready to eat. Leave them out of the shared takeout box if other people want their food unchanged. A small dish alongside lets everyone choose, and keeps you from dressing a whole order of rice with a green you've never tried on it.

You don't need to buy extra ingredients to make the greens belong. Try what you have with the meal you ordered. If the pairing works, remember it for another takeout night. If it doesn't, save that combination the trouble of a second attempt.

Next time the delivery bag lands on the counter, try a few washed shoots on one bite before you reach for another sauce packet.
