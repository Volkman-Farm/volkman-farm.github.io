From June through September it rains here most afternoons. Not a drizzle. The kind that empties in 40 minutes, floods a street corner, and is gone before you've found the umbrella.

A delivery operation in Central Florida either plans around that or spends half the year improvising.

The thing rain does not change is the cut. Greens come off the rack [at sunrise](/blog/harvest-morning-timeline/) regardless of what the sky is doing, because a tray that's ready is ready, and holding it a day to wait out weather costs more freshness than the weather ever could. The shed doesn't care. It's a roof and a rack and a light schedule.

What rain changes is the driving and the porch.

## The driving

The Friday route runs Seminole County in the morning, which is the half of the day Florida is most likely to leave alone. That wasn't designed as a weather decision, it came out of [the family calendar](/blog/why-we-stay-small-on-purpose/), but it turns out to be one. A morning route in summer beats an afternoon route roughly every time.

The evening runs into north Orlando are the ones that meet real weather, and the only answer there is that it takes longer. Standing water on Orange Blossom Trail, a light cycle that used to take one turn taking three. Nothing clever to say about it. We leave earlier.

## The porch

This is the part where you can help.

A clamshell is not waterproof and a porch is not always dry. Wind blows rain sideways under an overhang, and a step that's covered at noon isn't covered at 4. [We've written about the porch clock before](/blog/when-the-bag-lands-on-your-porch/), and rain is the version of that problem that moves fastest.

So when you order, tell us where the dry spot is. Behind the column, under the side awning, inside the screen door if it's unlocked, tucked against the wall on the left where the roof really reaches. Every house has one and you know yours. We don't, and standing on your step in a downpour is a bad time to be figuring it out.

Point us at the dry corner when you order, and then go stand on your own porch the next time it rains and check whether you were right about it. Most people are wrong by about 2 feet.
