Our greens go out in a 64 oz clamshell. They're priced at $3.50 an ounce.

Do that math and you get $224 for a box. Nobody's paying that, and nobody's getting 64 ounces of microgreens on their porch, because those 2 numbers are measuring different things.

## 2 ounces

**The 64 on the clamshell is a fluid ounce.** That's volume. It's how much space is inside the box, the same unit as a measuring cup. 64 fluid ounces is half a gallon.

**The $3.50 is per ounce of weight.** That's what goes on the scale.

Greens are mostly air by volume. A loose handful of [pea tendrils](/greens/pea/) takes up a lot of room and weighs very little, and a pile of [arugula](/greens/arugula/) is even lighter for its size. A box that holds half a gallon of water holds a lot less than half a gallon's weight in greens.

## Why it's sold by weight

Because weight is the fair unit for something this light. Two people could fill the same box, one packing it down and one letting it fall in loose, and get very different amounts of food. A scale doesn't care how it was packed.

[Every tray gets weighed](/blog/every-tray-gets-weighed/) on the way in, seed before it's sown. On the way out it's the same idea: [cut, into the clamshell, onto the scale](/blog/a-day-of-jobs-who-does-what/). What you pay for is what the scale read.

Leave them loose once they're home, too. Microgreens bruise when they're pressed, and a crushed leaf goes downhill fast in the fridge. Don't pack them down into a smaller container to save shelf space.

Next time you get a clamshell, put it on your kitchen scale before you open it, then weigh the empty box when you're done. The difference is what you bought, and it should match what you [ordered](/order/).
