The greens are gone. What's left is a clear box with a hinged lid, about 8 inches by 8 and a little over 3 deep.

We don't collect them back. It's ours on the drive and yours once it's on the porch.

## What it's made of

It's a [64 oz clamshell](/blog/a-64-oz-clamshell-sold-by-the-ounce/) made of PET, the same plastic family as a water bottle. At least 30% of it is post-consumer recycled PET, so some of it has been something else already. PET's resin code is 1.

The maker lists it as recyclable "under some recycling programs." Read the word *some* there. A clamshell and a bottle can be the same plastic and still be made and sorted differently, and not every program that takes one takes the other.

If you keep it, keep it cold. The maker built it for cold use, so the fridge is fine and hot food and the microwave aren't.

## Will your bin take it

[Seminole County](https://www.seminolecountyfl.gov/departments-services/environmental-services/solid-waste-management/recycling) takes "plastic bottles, cups, and containers" curbside, rinsed, dry, and loose in the bin. The [City of Orlando](https://www.orlando.gov/Trash-Recycling/What-Goes-Where) takes bottles and containers "empty and clean."

Neither list names clamshells. So we won't tell you yours does.

Whichever way it goes, it's held greens, so wash it out and let it dry before it goes in a bin.

Tonight, before it goes anywhere, look up your own city's list. If clamshells aren't on it, call the number on that page and ask about them by name.
