We have written a lot about how the route works and not much about the places on it. That seems backward, so we are fixing it. Over the next few weeks we want to introduce the nine neighborhoods properly, a couple at a time, starting with the two closest to our own front door.

[Sanford](/delivery/sanford/) and [Lake Mary](/delivery/lake-mary/) come first for a reason that is not sentiment. They are the shortest clock we run.

## Sanford is where we actually are

Sanford is not a delivery zone to us. It is the return address.

The shed is here. The trays are here. The chickens are here, and so is the front three-quarter acre we spent this summer seeding. Everything you read on this blog about sand that will not hold water, about a shed that fights a Florida July, about a flock that eats the spent trays, happened inside the Sanford city limits. When we say a green was grown a few miles from your door, for a Sanford customer that is often closer to a few minutes.

That does something to the delivery that is hard to replicate anywhere else on the list. On a Friday the first Sanford porches get greens that came off the rack maybe forty minutes earlier. They have not been anywhere. They went from a tray to a clamshell to a cooler bag to a step, and the longest leg of that trip was the driveway.

We cover the whole town: historic downtown around First Street and the Riverwalk, the Mayfair Market area, the Lake Mary Boulevard corridor, and the neighborhoods that ring Lake Monroe. If you live here, odds are good we are already on your side of town on Friday morning.

There is a second thing about Sanford worth saying plainly. A fair number of our regulars here cook for a living. Downtown has a real restaurant scene for a town this size, and people who spend their week in a kitchen tend to have opinions about produce. They are a demanding first audience and we are better for it. It is one thing to sell a stranger a clamshell. It is another to hand it to somebody who will notice if the pea shoots are a day tired.

## Lake Mary is the first turn out of the driveway

[Lake Mary](/delivery/lake-mary/) sits right next door, and it is the first stop rather than the second town. The Friday route runs south out of Sanford and Lake Mary catches it while the cooler bag is still cold from the shed.

We get all through it: Heathrow, the neighborhoods along Markham Woods Road, Greenwood Lakes, Timacuan, and the streets between Lake Mary Boulevard and Rinehart. Different neighborhoods with pretty different characters, all inside the same fifteen minutes of driving, which is exactly the kind of density a small route wants.

Lake Mary is also where the standing-order habit took hold first. Something about a Friday morning drop lines up with how people here shop. Greens land before lunch, the weekend is right there, and dinner Friday night uses the tender stuff while it is at its absolute best.

## Why these two are the easy ones

We have said before that [the routes are a family calendar](/blog/do-you-deliver-to-my-street/) rather than a sales territory. Monday and Wednesday evenings exist because of taekwondo and rock climbing. Sunday afternoon exists because church is in Parramore. Friday morning is the exception. Nothing else is pulling us south on a Friday. That route exists purely because it is home, and it is the only one where the driving is the point instead of a byproduct.

That is why the Friday clock is so short. Everywhere else on the route map, greens ride a while before the first porch. Here they barely ride at all. We wrote up [what a harvest morning looks like](/blog/harvest-morning-timeline/) and the whole design of it is to keep that first hour short. In Sanford and Lake Mary the first hour is not a design problem. It is just geography doing us a favor.

If you are in either town and we have not met yet, [start an order](/order/) and pick your Friday. We are probably closer than you think.

Winter Springs and Oviedo are up next, and they round out the same route.
