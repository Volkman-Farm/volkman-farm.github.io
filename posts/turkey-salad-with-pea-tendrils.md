A container of leftover turkey can feed you for several lunches, but there's no need to decide what all of it will be at once. Scoop out enough for lunch and chop it while it's cold. A spoonful of mayonnaise, a little Dijon and some diced celery make a good turkey salad. Taste it before you add salt, especially if the turkey was brined.

[Pea tendrils](/greens/pea/) add a sweet snap that works with the celery. Rinse the portion you're using, pat it dry and cut the long shoots into pieces about an inch long. Fold a small handful into the bowl once you've mixed the dressing. Leaving the shoots long makes them harder to scoop and more likely to pull out of a sandwich on the first bite.

Try a spoonful before you add more. If the salad tastes flat, a squeeze of lemon will help more than another handful of greens. If you like the pea flavor but want more crunch, save a few short shoots to scatter over your serving just before you eat.

Use turkey that was promptly chilled. [USDA's leftovers guidance](https://ask.fsis.usda.gov/article/How-do-I-handle-leftovers-safely) says to refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours (1 hour above 90°F) and use them within 3 to 4 days. Count from when you cooked the turkey, not from when you mixed the salad.
