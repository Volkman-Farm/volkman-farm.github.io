It's football season, which in Central Florida means it's still 90 degrees.

A board of food on a tailgate or a folding table sits out for hours. Cheese sweats. Crackers go soft. And a pile of microgreens scattered over the top looks great for about 20 minutes, then slumps into a wet green film on the meat.

Greens can make a snack board better. They just can't be the part you lay out first and leave.

## Pick the sturdy ones

On a board that lives outdoors, stems matter more than leaves.

**[Pea tendrils](/greens/pea/)** are the sturdiest thing we grow. Thick stems, real body, and they hold their shape in heat longer than anything else on the rack. **[Broccoli](/greens/broccoli/)** and **[kale](/greens/kale/)** are the next tier: small leaves on stems that stand up for a while.

Leave **cilantro**, **arugula**, and **beet** for the indoor version of this. They're tender, and they'll be gone before the first quarter is.

## Tuck them in, don't scatter them

A loose pile on top of a board is fully exposed on every side. Greens tucked inside something last a lot longer.

**Sliders.** A pinch of pea or broccoli under the top bun. The bun is shade and the meat is a floor.

**Pinwheels.** Roll them into a tortilla with cream cheese and ham, slice, and the greens are sealed in a spiral. [The school lunch post](/blog/school-lunches-with-a-secret/) covers the packing logic.

**Pimento cheese or any thick dip.** Press a handful into the top so the stems stand up in it. The dip anchors them and keeps them upright for longer than you'd think.

## Keep a cold bowl

The best move is the simplest. Set a small bowl of greens inside a bigger bowl of ice, next to the board, and let people add them to whatever they grabbed. The greens stay cold, the board stays dry, and nobody gets a wilted handful.

Our clamshells say wash before using, so do that at home the night before. Wash, dry them properly, and pack them in a sealed container in the cooler. Wet greens in a hot parking lot go downhill fast.

This Saturday, build the board the way you normally would and put the greens in a bowl on ice beside it. Check the bowl at the end of the day and see what's left. That tells you exactly how many to bring next week.
