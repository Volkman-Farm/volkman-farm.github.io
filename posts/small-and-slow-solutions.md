2 birds spent 5 weeks in a box built for chicks, and we couldn't do anything to make it 4.

They came out on Oct 4 and [went back in with the flock](/blog/a-quiet-week-in-the-run/) without any trouble. While they were in there, the whole plan for next year sat behind them: no chicks until the brooder was empty, no second tractor until the chicks.

David Holmgren's ninth principle fits that box better than anything else on his list. He lists 12, and this one says: use small and slow solutions. The proverb he pins to it is the bigger they are, the harder they fall. Small systems are easier to keep up, they use what's on hand, and when one fails it doesn't take everything else down with it.

## The ones we didn't choose

Most of what's slow on this property wasn't a decision.

The shed is [one room with one rack](/blog/the-shed-albert-built/). That's what I built, and it holds what it holds. A tray of cilantro takes 18 to 21 days in summer and [longer now that it's October](/blog/the-shed-in-october/). You can't hurry it. You can only run more trays, and only until the rack's full.

The routes are slow in a different way. They run on drives the family was [already making](/blog/why-we-stay-small-on-purpose/). Friday morning in Seminole County, Monday and Wednesday evenings in north Orlando, Sunday afternoon downtown. That caps them, and the cap was there before the first tray.

The well and the cistern are slow because [they're not happening next year](/blog/what-were-designing-for-next-year/). The low spots will keep holding water for 2 or 3 days after a hard storm and we'll keep watching where.

None of that is wisdom. It's just the size we are.

## The ones we could choose

The front field is different. Three-quarters of an acre, mowed again and waiting, and [no plan](/blog/back-with-the-flock/) for what comes next.

The fast solution is to pick something and plant all of it. The slow one is to try a strip. One mix down one side, see what the sand does with it over a season, and leave the rest mowed.

Trees are the same. We'll plant some next year, and we haven't picked any. The contours already say the trees go high and the water-tolerant plants go low. A slow start there is 2 or 3 trees on the high ground, not a whole guild on day one. If one dies, we learn something about that corner and we haven't lost a row.

The principle isn't a rule for people with no money and no time, though it fits them fine. It's a way to be wrong cheaply.

## The test

Holmgren's not saying never build anything big. He's saying make the small version first and let it tell you whether the big one's worth it.

So pick one project on your list for next year and ask what the smallest working version of it would be. One bed instead of a garden. One tree instead of an orchard. One nest box. Build that one first, and give it a full season before you decide what the second one looks like.
