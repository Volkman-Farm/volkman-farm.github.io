Dinner can be nearly ready for quite a while. The potatoes need another few minutes, someone hasn't made it home yet, and you're hungry enough to start eating ingredients. A cracker with a little cream cheese is a reasonable place to start.

A few microgreens fit there, too. The cheese gives the stems something to stick to, so you can pick it up without leaving most of the topping on the plate. You don't need enough greens to hide the cracker. A small pinch is easier to eat.

[Arugula](/greens/arugula/) works if you like a peppery bite alongside the cheese. [Broccoli](/greens/broccoli/) is milder. Either way, a fairly plain cracker leaves more room to notice the green than one already covered in garlic and seasoning. That doesn't make the seasoned cracker wrong. You may just taste less of what you put on it.

There's no need to assemble a whole tray. Make a couple, eat them, and see whether you want another. If you're sharing, leave the rest of the crackers dry and let people add their own topping. They stay crisp longer that way, and you haven't turned a small snack into another dish that needs finishing before dinner.

Wash the greens under running water and dry them with a clean towel before using them. [FDA guidance](https://www.fda.gov/food/buy-store-serve-safe-food/selecting-and-serving-produce-safely) advises against soap or produce wash. Put the remaining greens and cream cheese back in the refrigerator while you get on with cooking.

You can do this with the greens you already have. The bag doesn't have to wait until you're making a salad.
