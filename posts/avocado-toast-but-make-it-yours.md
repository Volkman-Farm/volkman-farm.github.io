Avocado toast is the pairing everybody thinks of first, which is exactly why it's usually done badly. The greens go on as a photograph. 3 tendrils laid across the top, nothing underneath them, and they fall off on the first bite.

The fix is to stop treating them as a garnish and start treating them as the salad course on top of the bread.

Mash the avocado rougher than looks right. A smooth spread has nothing for the greens to hold onto, and a chunky one grabs them. Salt it, acid it, then pile the greens on in a real handful and press down gently with the back of the fork. That press seats the stems into the avocado so the thing is structurally one object instead of two.

Then eat it immediately, because avocado starts going grey the moment it meets air and greens start wilting the moment they meet salt.

## Which one to use

**Arugula** if you want the toast to taste different than it did last week. Pepper against fat is one of the oldest good ideas in cooking, and avocado is nothing but fat.

**Pea tendrils** if you want it sweet and substantial. They have enough body to hold their shape under the press, and they turn the toast into something closer to a meal.

**Mustard** if you're brave, and go light. It'll fight the avocado rather than sit beside it, which some mornings is what you want.

**Broccoli or kale** if the toast is for somebody who is not sure about any of this yet.

Cilantro is the odd one. It's excellent here but it takes over, so it belongs on a version with lime and chili rather than a version with lemon and pepper. Pick one direction.

Egg on top is not a complication. It's the same rule as everything else: greens go on after the egg, not under it, because [heat is what kills them](/blog/soups-get-their-finish/) and a runny yolk is hot enough to matter.

Tomorrow morning, make it the way you always do, then put twice as many greens on as feels correct and press them in.
