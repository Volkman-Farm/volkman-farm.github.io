Seed goes on the scale before it goes on the soil. Every tray, every time.

It takes about 10 seconds and it is the least interesting part of a seeding morning. It is also the step we are strictest about, because seed is [the one input you cannot fix later](/blog/seed-sourcing-who-we-buy-from/). Everything else in the shed gives you a second chance. Light can be raised. Water can be adjusted tomorrow. A tray that was seeded wrong was wrong before it ever went dark, and you find out on day 8.

## What the number is protecting

**Too thin** is the boring failure. Gaps. The mat never closes, plants stand up alone instead of holding each other, and you cut a tray that yields less than it should have for the same 10 days of light and water. Nothing dies. You wasted the expensive part, which is the rack space and the time, not the seed.

**Too dense** is the one that costs you. Seed packed shoulder to shoulder holds moisture against itself, air stops moving through the canopy, and you get damping off, which is one of the ways [a tray goes wrong](/blog/when-a-tray-goes-wrong/) in a humid Florida shed. A slightly thin tray is a smaller harvest. A tray seeded too heavy is sometimes no harvest.

What you want is a bed where the seed sits in one layer, touching, not stacked. You can see that in a finished tray. You cannot see it in a handful.

## Every variety has its own number

[Cilantro seed](/greens/cilantro/) is a big cracked husk. Pea is bigger still and goes for an overnight soak before it ever hits soil. [Arugula](/greens/arugula/) is close to dust.

Put the same even coverage on 3 trays of those and you have weighed out 3 very different amounts. There is no single number to memorize, which is the argument for the scale rather than against it. The scale is how you keep 10 different right answers straight without trusting your hand to remember which variety it is holding.

The same scale comes back out at harvest, where the number matters for a different reason. We sell by the ounce, and a customer is owed exactly what they paid for.

## If you are growing at home

Weigh the first tray of a new variety, then write the number on the bag in marker. Seed bags do not come with your tray, your soil depth, or your shed, and the number that works in your setup is worth more to you than the number that works in ours.

Then weigh the next one anyway.
