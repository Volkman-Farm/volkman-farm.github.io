A microgreen gets about a week.

Not a week from when it reaches you. A week from the morning it's cut, and the leaf doesn't care what happens to it in between. We [wrote about that clock](/blog/how-long-do-microgreens-last/) in July, and it's the reason there's no shipping option anywhere on this site.

Walk a box through it. Cut Monday morning, packed by noon, picked up that afternoon. Ground shipping inside Florida is a day or 2 if nothing goes sideways. Then it sits on a porch, and a porch in Sanford at 3 in the afternoon is not a fridge. By the time somebody lifts the lid it's day 3 of 7, sometimes day 4, and the first few days are the best ones a green has.

You can fight that with packaging. Ice packs, insulated liners, overnight service. It works for a while. It also costs more to ship 2 ounces overnight than the 2 ounces cost, and you're paying for a cold chain to protect something that didn't need protecting until you put it in a box.

## What we do instead

Greens get cut, go into a clamshell, and the clamshell goes into an insulated cooler bag in [the back of the car](/blog/the-car-is-the-warehouse/). The bag holds its temperature for a morning. It doesn't have to hold it any longer, because the drive is measured in minutes and the bag [comes home with us](/blog/when-the-bag-lands-on-your-porch/).

So the reach of this farm is a drive, not a zip code table. [9 neighborhoods](/delivery/) on 3 routes, all of them close enough that the last stop gets greens in about the same shape as the first.

Some growers ship microgreens and do a good job of it. They build their whole operation around the box: the harvest timing, the packaging, the carrier schedule. We built ours around a car and a cooler bag. A short trip is the one thing we can do better than anybody with a box.

## If you're outside the map

Find the person growing near you. Microgreens are a small-scale crop by nature, and there's usually somebody with a shed and a rack closer than you'd guess.

Ask them one question before you buy: what morning was this cut? If they can tell you, they're keeping the same clock we are. If they can't, you've learned something about the tray.
