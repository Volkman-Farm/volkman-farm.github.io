A few mustard shoots can bring a sharp bite to mashed potatoes. Someone at the table may want more. Someone else may want the potatoes left alone.

Put the greens beside the serving dish and let people [top their own plates](/blog/thanksgiving-greens-go-on-last/). Dinner can stay the same for everyone up to that point.

If you're choosing 2 varieties, give them different flavors. [Broccoli micros](/greens/broccoli/) are mild. [Mustard](/greens/mustard/) has a sharper, horseradish-like bite. [Arugula](/greens/arugula/) brings pepper too, so broccoli and arugula would also give the table a choice. You don't need all 3.

Use a small bowl for each variety, with its own clean serving tongs. Put the names beside them. “Broccoli, mild” tells someone more than “the green one.” “Mustard, sharp” gives them a reason to start with a few shoots. Someone who likes both can mix them on their own plate.

Keep cilantro separate if you know it's a dividing line. It can go beside tacos, beans, or guacamole without being stirred through the entire bowl. And if somebody wants no greens, pass the food along.

Dressing can stay separate too. Let each person add [a little at a time](/blog/a-little-dressing-first/), especially if they're trying a green they haven't eaten before. Ask about food allergies before planning the meal, and make the greens and dressing ingredients clear to your guests.

Our clamshells say wash before using. At home, [wash the portion you're serving](/blog/how-do-you-wash-just-a-handful/) under running water, then dry it with a clean towel or paper towel. Use clean hands and utensils. Leave the rest dry in the fridge.

Set out small portions close to mealtime and keep the reserves refrigerated. For a longer buffet, nest the serving bowls in ice and keep the greens at 40°F or below. Replace a nearly empty bowl with a clean, freshly filled one instead of topping it up.

The [FDA's buffet guide](https://www.fda.gov/food/buy-store-serve-safe-food/serving-safe-buffets) says to discard perishable food left at room temperature for more than 2 hours, or 1 hour when the temperature is above 90°F. A leaf that still looks crisp hasn't proved it's safe to keep.

Before the next shared meal, set 2 small bowls beside the serving dish. Leave room for people to choose.
