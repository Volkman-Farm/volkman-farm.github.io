Microgreens often get photographed whole, with the stems and leaves arranged where you can see them. That doesn't mean you have to eat them that way.

If a long shoot pulls out of your sandwich when you take a bite, you can cut it shorter. You don't need to choose a different green or rebuild lunch around it. A few cuts with a clean knife will do.

[Pea tendrils](/greens/pea/) are a good example. The curls look nice on a plate, but a shorter piece may be easier to tuck into a sandwich. You still get the stem and the leaf. They just won't reach quite so far beyond the bread.

The same goes for mixing greens into something soft. A few chopped shoots can spread through cream cheese more easily than a tangle of whole ones. If you're adding them to a dip, think about how much will fit on the cracker. Leave the pieces large enough to notice if you like the texture. Cut them smaller if you'd prefer them mixed through.

Wash the portion you're going to eat and dry it before cutting, as you would before serving it whole. Use a clean board and knife. Leave the rest of the greens in the fridge until you're ready for them.

We wouldn't bother chopping every little broccoli shoot for a salad. Sometimes the greens are already the size you want, and there's no reason to add another job. But if you like the flavor and find the length awkward, it's worth trying a few shorter pieces before deciding that variety isn't for you.
