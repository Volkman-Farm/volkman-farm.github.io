There were turkeys in the front field again.

3 to 5 of them, which is how many there usually are. They come every few weeks, wander the field for a while, and leave. Nobody here does anything about them. They were already doing this when we moved in, and they've kept doing it through everything we've done to that ground since.

Which is a lot, if you've been reading along. That front 3/4 of an acre has been sunn hemp, then [chopped sunn hemp](/blog/chop-and-drop/), then a mowed field, then a 50 lb bag of [cover crop mix broadcast over the residue](/blog/the-summer-garden-that-feeds-the-fall-one/), then [a green field where the sand used to be](/blog/cover-crop-check-in/). The turkeys have walked every version of it. As far as I can tell they've never once had an opinion about our plans.

## The show

The reason you notice them is the display.

A tom fans out. Tail up in a full half circle, wings dropped and dragging, body puffed until he's twice the bird he was a minute ago, and then he walks. Slowly. Back and forth, in front of whichever birds he's performing for. It's ridiculous and it's magnificent at the same time.

I'd always read this as courtship, and it is. But the bird books put Florida's breeding season in spring, with hens laying in March and April, and this is September. It turns out a tom will fan for more than one reason. It's also how he tells the other birds where he ranks, and in Florida, where it never really gets cold, the season runs looser than it does up north. So a September strut is either a bird getting an early start or a bird making a point. From the edge of the field I can't tell you which.

These are Osceola turkeys, by the way. That's the subspecies that lives in peninsular Florida and nowhere else in the world, which means the birds walking our front field are about as local as anything on this property. More local than us. Much more local than the lemon tree.

## What they're doing out there

Probably eating, between performances.

What a turkey eats in an open field this time of year is seed, insects, and green shoots, and that front field has all 3 standing in it right now. I haven't watched close enough to tell you what they're picking at.

We didn't plant that field for turkeys. We planted it for the ground, so the sand would get covered and something would be growing roots down into it instead of cooking in the sun. The turkeys kept coming when it was sunn hemp, kept coming when it was stubble, and they're still coming now. Whatever brings them back, it was here before any of it.

They're not ours and they never will be. We'll keep the paddocks for the birds that are, and the shed out back for [the greens](/greens/).

Next time you drive past an open field around here, slow down and look along the edges. If you see a bird that looks too big and too slow to be real, it's real. Give it a minute. It might fan out.
