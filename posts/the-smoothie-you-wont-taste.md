A smoothie is the easiest hiding place in a kitchen. It is cold, it is already sweet, and it is opaque. We said in passing [when we wrote up broccoli](/blog/broccoli-micros-do-the-heavy-lifting/) that it vanishes into one completely. Here is the actual method, because there are three ways to get this wrong and all three are easy to avoid.

## Start smaller than you think

A small handful is plenty for one glass. Broccoli micros are tender the whole way through with no real stem, so a handful goes further than the same volume of mature greens would. An ounce covers most of a week of breakfasts.

Wash the portion you are about to use and leave the rest of the clamshell dry in the fridge. That order is what keeps the greens standing for the week.

Going heavy is the mistake that gets you caught, and not because of flavor. Broccoli barely has one. It is volume that turns a smoothie grassy at the finish, so the fix is to use less than feels right and work up from there.

## The color is what gives you away

Flavor is not really the risk. Color is. Green plus red fruit makes a muddy brown, and a brown smoothie gets turned down on sight, before anyone has tasted anything.

Two ways around that. Go dark, with blueberries or cherries or blackberries, which take the green along with them and land somewhere purple that reads as intentional. Or go all the way green, with banana and mango and pineapple. Strawberry and raspberry are the two to skip.

## Order in the jar

Liquid first, then the greens, then the frozen fruit on top.

The blade needs something thin to move before it will pull anything down, and the frozen fruit riding last is what drives the greens into the blades instead of letting them pack up against the lid. Get the order backward and you get green flecks stuck around the rim, which defeats the whole point.

Blending breaks a leaf down much the way chewing does. We handled what that does and does not let us claim in the spotlight post, with the limits stated plainly, and we are not going to add anything to it here.

## The part we will not oversell

This gets greens into someone who has firm opinions about greens, and it works. It is not a substitute for a vegetable showing up visible on a plate at dinner, and we would not pretend otherwise. It buys you a good breakfast, not a settled argument.
