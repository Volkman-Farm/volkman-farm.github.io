"Cut this morning." We say it on the porch and we mean it literally. It is the one line that separates our greens from a plastic tub that has been sitting at the store, and it is worth slowing down on, because a fair amount of care is packed into three words. Here is what a harvest morning actually looks like, and why the timing is not a slogan. It is the whole product.

We already covered [why the clock on a green starts at the cut](/blog/how-long-do-microgreens-last/), not the sale, so we will not repeat it. The short version: a microgreen is at its best the hour it leaves the tray, and every hour after that is downhill. So the whole morning is built to keep that first hour short.

## Why not the night before

The obvious question is why we do not cut the evening before and get a head start. The greens answer that one for you. A tray cut Thursday night and held overnight, even cold, is already a day into its decline before it ever reaches a porch. Cutting Friday at first light means the greens spend their best hours in your fridge instead of ours.

There is a second reason, and it is about the plant itself. Greens are crispest in the cool of the morning. Overnight they take up water and firm up, and the shed has not started to heat yet. Cut a tray at sunrise and the leaves are stiff and standing. Cut that same tray at two in the afternoon in a Florida July and they are softer before the knife even touches them. Morning is simply when the crop is holding itself best.

## The cut itself

The actual harvest is faster and plainer than people expect. Every tray on the Friday list comes off the rack. We run a clean blade straight across, just above the soil line, high enough to leave the roots and the spent seed behind and low enough not to waste stem. The greens fall into the 64 oz clamshell, and we weigh as we go so each one lands at its ounce without a second handling.

Nothing gets a rinse. The blade is the last thing that touches the crop. What grew in the tray is exactly what goes in the clamshell: cut, weighed, closed. That is not a shortcut, it is the rule. Our label says wash before using because that step belongs to you, at the plate, on the handful you are about to eat.

The order of the trays matters too. The tender ones, [cilantro](/greens/cilantro/) and the fine leaf, get cut last so they sit the shortest. The sturdy [kale](/greens/kale/) and pea can go first. By the time the sun is fully up the rack is emptier and the cooler bag is full.

## Everything after is just protecting it

From there it is the part we have written about before: pack the bag, text the route, and drive the [Seminole County loop](/blog/why-we-deliver-to-porches/) nearest to farthest so nothing rides warm. But the piece that makes "cut this morning" honest happens before any of that, in a shed at first light with a blade and an order sheet. Everything downstream is just protecting the head start.

That is what those three words buy you: greens that are actually as young as they look, with the whole week still ahead of them in your kitchen instead of behind them in a truck.
