The air conditioner is running less this month. In July it barely got a break.

That's one of 3 things October changes in the shed.

**The A/C runs less.** In July we wrote about [keeping the shed cool](/blog/keeping-a-grow-shed-cool/) when everything outside the walls was working against us, and last week we told the story of [the hose problem](/blog/12-weeks-in-taking-stock/) that made it harder than it had to be. October takes some of that load off. The 2-hose setup is the same one that's been in there since the fix. It just has less to fight.

**The air is drier.** Humidity in the shed is down. Summer here is wet air coming at the building from every side, and that eases up as the season turns.

**Grow times are longer.** Every tray is taking more days to reach the knife than it did in summer.

That last one matters most to anyone ordering, because the day counts on our variety pages were written in July. [Cilantro](/greens/cilantro/) at 18 to 21 days, [kale](/greens/kale/) at 8 to 12, [beet](/greens/beet/) at about 14. Those are summer clocks. In October they run long.

## What it does to an order

We seed from what we know is coming. A tray that needs a few more days needs a few more days of notice, and that stacks up fastest on the slow ones. Cilantro was already the slowest thing on the rack. [Our holiday post](/blog/holiday-greens-start-in-november/) counted back from Thanksgiving using the summer number.

So the earlier you tell us, the more room there is for the clock to run long without anyone noticing.

If there's a date this fall you want greens for, a dinner, a party, a holiday, text us the date now and what you'd want. We'll count back from it with October numbers.
