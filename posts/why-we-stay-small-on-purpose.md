Every business article you will ever read assumes you want to be bigger. More accounts, more volume, more trucks, a second location. Growth is treated as the only direction a thing can move that is not failure.

We built this one the other way around. The routes came out of a calendar that already existed, and the size of the farm is whatever fits inside it.

## The calendar came first

There are three routes.

Friday morning runs through Seminole County, which is home. Sanford, Lake Mary, Winter Springs, Oviedo, Casselberry. That one exists because Friday morning is the quietest stretch of our week and because everything on it is close to the [shed](/blog/the-shed-albert-built/) the greens are cut in.

Monday and Wednesday evenings run north Orlando. Those two nights are taekwondo and rock climbing. We are already in the car, already driving that direction, already parked somewhere for an hour waiting on a kid. The route is built on top of a drive that was happening anyway.

Sunday afternoon runs downtown, because church is in Parramore and we are already there.

Read that back and notice what it is not. It is not a coverage map drawn by picking the densest zip codes. It is a week that a family of five already had, with deliveries laid into the gaps.

That is the actual answer to why we are small. Not modesty, not a philosophy about slow food. The greens go where we were already going.

## What the ceiling is made of

Two things cap this, and neither of them is ambition.

The first is the shed. It is one room with one rack of lights and one [portable air conditioner](/blog/keeping-a-grow-shed-cool/) fighting a Florida summer. A tray takes the days it takes, the rack holds what it holds, and no amount of wanting more changes either number. You cannot hurry a crop that is eight days from seed to cut. You can only run more trays, and only until the rack is full.

The second is time on a Friday morning. A route is not just driving. It is cutting, packing, loading a cooler bag, and getting to porches while everything is still cold. Add enough stops and you are not running a longer route, you are running a worse one, because the last porch of the morning gets greens that have been riding around since sunrise. There is a point where more stops makes the product worse, and that point arrives well before the day runs out of hours.

## What we would give up

The honest version of the anti scale argument is that scaling is not evil, it is a trade, and here is the trade we would be making.

Cut to porch time is the entire pitch. Greens cut off a rack in Sanford at six in the morning and set on a porch four towns away before lunch are a genuinely different product from greens cut in another state, then boxed, trucked, warehoused, and shelved. That gap is the only real advantage a farm this size has over a distributor, and every unit of scale eats into it. A bigger operation needs a cold chain, a schedule with buffers in it, and eventually a middleman. All three of those exist to absorb time, and time is the thing we are selling.

So when we say small on purpose, that is what we mean. We are not protecting a lifestyle. We are protecting the one number that makes any of this worth doing.

## The part that is not romantic

Small has real costs and we are not going to pretend otherwise.

It means the [nine neighborhoods](/delivery/) we deliver to are the nine that fit those three drives, and if you are outside them the honest answer is not yet rather than sure, we will figure something out. It means the price per ounce is what it is, because there is no volume underneath it doing the work that volume does. It means a week where the family calendar changes is a week the route has to change with it.

We would rather own those tradeoffs than the other set. A farm that grows past the family it belongs to is not the same farm anymore. It is just a smaller version of the thing we were trying to be an alternative to.

If your street is on one of those three drives, [that is what the order page is for](/order/). If it is not, tell us anyway. Routes get redrawn when the calendar does.
