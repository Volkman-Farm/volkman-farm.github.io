On Sunday we moved 2 birds out of the brooder and put them in with the rest of the flock.

They're the 2 that were hurt at the end of August. They went into the brooder [that week](/blog/we-were-watching-the-sky/), they were [still in it 2 weeks later](/blog/two-weeks-and-nothing-has-come-back/), and a week ago I wrote that [most of next year was waiting on them](/blog/what-were-designing-for-next-year/). They took their time. 5 weeks in a box built for chicks.

Now the box is empty.

The run they walked into isn't the one they left. It's the rebuilt one: 2 paddocks, 2 lines of electric netting with a corridor between them, an aviary net over the top.

## What the empty brooder means

The plan we published last week said we'd order chicks as soon as the brooder was empty. Not before.

That's changed a little. Next week is our wedding anniversary, so we'll plan the chicks after that. Same order as before: chicks, then a second chicken tractor. Just a week later than the sentence we put in print.

## The front field

The cover crop got mowed again last week.

That front three-quarter acre has had a busy year. Sunn hemp in the spring, then Hancock's summer mix broadcast over it on Aug 1, which [came up even](/blog/cover-crop-check-in/), and now another mowing.

What we don't have is a next step. There's no solid plan for what goes in that field after this.

So we're asking. If you've worked a field like this in Central Florida, poorly drained fine sand, full sun, coming off a summer cover crop, what would you do with it this fall and winter? A cool season mix? Leave it be? Something we haven't thought of?

Email us at [micros@volkman.farm](mailto:micros@volkman.farm). If you've done it on your own ground, tell us what happened. That's worth more to us than what a seed bag says.
