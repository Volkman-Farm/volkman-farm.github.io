[Cilantro microgreens](/greens/cilantro/) can go on the tacos, salsa, and guacamole you already make. They've got the cilantro flavor, with finer stems than a mature bunch. Use the tender shoots where you'd have used chopped leaves.

Wash the portion you're about to eat, the way our clamshell label says, and dry it. Start with a little, taste, and add more if you want it. Dinner can keep going.

Other swaps change the flavor. [Pea tendrils](/greens/pea/) bring sweetness and crunch. [Arugula](/greens/arugula/) brings pepper. Either could go on a sandwich where you'd planned to put herbs, if you like the sound of that with the filling. Neither will taste like parsley or basil.

For a spoonful of salsa or something fresh on top of a bowl, it's easy to adjust as you go. A packed cup of chopped herbs is a different job. A loose handful of shoots won't measure the same, so don't assume you can swap them cup for cup.

If you're making a blended sauce, our [pesto and green sauce post](/blog/pesto-and-other-things-you-blend/) has places to start. Taste a small batch before you commit the rest of the greens.

Keep the rosemary or thyme that a recipe asks you to simmer in the pot. Microgreens added at the table won't give the soup that seasoning. They can go on the finished bowl too, as in our [soup guide](/blog/soups-get-their-finish/).

Next time you make guacamole, try cilantro micros in one spoonful before adding them to the bowl. You'll know whether you want more.
