A few pea shoots inside a hot, crisp quesadilla give the cheese something fresh to play against.

The first bite of a quesadilla is usually the best one. The tortilla still cracks a little, and the cheese hasn't had time to go stiff. It's a good place to try a few pea shoots, as long as you don't put them in the pan.

Make the quesadilla the way you normally do. Once it's off the heat, lift the top of one wedge and lay a few shoots near the open edge. Set the tortilla back down without pressing hard. You'll catch the fresh pea taste right away instead of having to hunt for it under the cheese.

If the stems keep pulling out in one long piece, cut them shorter next time. Kitchen scissors does the job right over the plate. And if you're using a sharp cheddar, give it one bite before reaching for salsa. You might like what the peas do on their own.

Leave the rest of the wedges alone until somebody asks. A quesadilla is a pretty forgiving place to try something new at lunch, especially when there's a plain one right beside it.
