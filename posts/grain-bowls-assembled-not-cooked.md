By the middle of August nobody in this house wants to turn the stove on at six o'clock. The kitchen is already the warmest room we have, and adding a pot to it feels like an argument you are going to lose.

So dinner stops being something you cook and starts being something you assemble.

The one thing worth cooking is the grain, and you cook it once. Rice, farro, barley, quinoa, whatever you like. Make a lot of it early in the week, in the morning if you can, when the house is still cool and running a burner costs you nothing in comfort. Then it goes in the fridge and it is done being a chore. Every bowl after that is just opening containers.

Cold grain is not a consolation prize either. Warm rice steams whatever you put on it, which is exactly what you do not want when the top of the bowl is delicate. Chilled grain firms up and stays separate, and it holds a dressing better than hot grain does because it is not busy giving off water.

The shape of a bowl is four things and then the greens.

A base, which is the grain. A protein you did not have to cook tonight: a can of chickpeas or white beans, leftover chicken from Sunday, a couple of hard boiled eggs, tinned fish, cubed feta if you want it easy. Something acidic, and this is the part people skip. Lemon, a splash of vinegar, quick pickled onion, anything sharp enough to keep the whole bowl from tasting flat and heavy. Then something fat to carry it: olive oil, tahini, yogurt, avocado.

Greens go on last, on top, right before it goes to the table. We made the long version of that case [back in the jar salad post](/blog/summer-salads-that-dont-wilt-by-noon/), so we will not run it twice. Same principle, different container.

For which ones, it depends what job you want them doing. [Pea tendrils](/greens/pea/) are the easy pick because they are sweet and they have real crunch, and they stand up to a heavier bowl without disappearing into it. Broccoli is the neutral one when the rest of the bowl already has a lot going on. Kohlrabi brings a clean crispness that plays well against beans. And if you want the bowl to actually bite back, a small pinch of mustard does it, though a small pinch is genuinely the instruction there.

Our label says wash before using, so give the handful a rinse right as it goes on.

The nice part is how little any of this asks of you at the end of a hot day. The grain is cooked, the beans came out of a can, and the only knife work is a cucumber. The greens are the one thing that makes it look like somebody tried.

[Ten varieties](/greens/) and any of them will sit on a bowl.
