The fastest green we grow is ready in about a week. The slowest takes 3.

Every tray in the shed is on its own clock, and the numbers below are the ones on our variety pages, all counted in summer. [Last week we wrote](/blog/the-shed-in-october/) that October is stretching every one of them. Treat each range as the short end for now.

## Fastest to slowest

**[Mustard](/greens/mustard/), 7 to 10 days.** The quickest thing on the shelf, and the heat peaks right at harvest.

**[Arugula](/greens/arugula/), about 8 days.** Close behind mustard, same family, and a lot of flavor for a leaf that young.

**[Broccoli](/greens/broccoli/), 8 to 10 days.** We cut it right as the first true leaves start to show.

**[Kale](/greens/kale/), 8 to 12 days.** Broccoli's twin [in the family tree](/blog/four-families-on-one-shelf/), with a couple more days on the long end.

**[Pea](/greens/pea/), 10 to 14 days.** The seed soaks overnight before it's sown, and we let it grow taller than the rest for the curl.

**[Beet](/greens/beet/), about 14 days.** Slow to wake up. The seed is a hard husk and gets an overnight soak too.

**[Cilantro](/greens/cilantro/), 18 to 21 days.** The slowest by a full week. The seed gets cracked before it goes down, and then it takes its time.

## What the spread means

4 of the 7 finish inside the same few days, and they're the 4 mustard-family greens. Then there's a gap, pea and beet in the middle, and cilantro off by itself at the end.

So an order of mustard and arugula is the easiest thing we can be asked for on short notice. An order with cilantro in it starts 3 weeks back, and in October a little more than that.

It's also why [the salad mix](/blog/whats-in-the-salad-mix/) is built the way it is. Greens that share a clock can share a tray. Cilantro can't share with anybody.

If there's a green you want for a specific day, find it on this list, count back from the date, and add a few days for the season. That's the day we'd need to know by.
