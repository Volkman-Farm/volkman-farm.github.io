The knife is a Mercer Culinary Millennia, the 6-inch produce knife with the green handle. It's a restaurant supply knife. Nothing about it was made for microgreens.

Every tray gets cut with it, and it's [mine to hold](/blog/a-day-of-jobs-who-does-what/).

## How low

As close to the soil as it'll go without taking any roots or dirt with it.

That's the rule for all 7 varieties. [Pea tendrils](/greens/pea/) get grown a little taller than the rest, [cilantro](/greens/cilantro/) sits in the tray for 3 weeks, and some trays get cut [before the first true leaves and some right as they show](/blog/cotyledon-or-true-leaf/). The height of the cut doesn't change for any of it.

Every bit of stem above the soil is something you eat. Below it is root and soil, and our greens go out [exactly as they come off the blade](/blog/harvest-morning-timeline/). So the cut sits right on the line between those 2 things, and the line is the same on every tray.

## What gets left

Roots, soil, the spent seed hulls, and a short stub of stem. That tray [doesn't grow back](/blog/do-microgreens-grow-back/). It goes out to the chickens.

## If you grow at home

Use the sharpest clean blade you own, and cut lower than feels right. Most people's first instinct is to leave a safety margin of stem, and that margin is food left in the tray.

Then look at what came off. If there's any soil in the pile, you went too low. If there's a forest of green stubs left standing, you went too high. Cut the next tray a little closer to wherever the line was.
