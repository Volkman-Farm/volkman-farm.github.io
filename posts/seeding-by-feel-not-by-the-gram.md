The bag has a recommended seeding rate printed on it. We don't weigh anything.

That's going to sound careless to anybody who has read one of the many microgreen guides that treat grams per tray as the difference between a crop and a failure. It isn't careless. It's what happens after you've done the same tray enough times that your hand knows the answer faster than a scale does.

The number is protecting against 2 things. Once you can see both, you can read a tray without measuring anything.

**Too thin** is the boring failure. Gaps. The mat never closes, individual plants stand up alone instead of holding each other, and you cut a tray that yields less than it should have for the same 10 days of light and water. Nothing dies. You just wasted the expensive part, which is the time and the rack space, not the seed.

**Too dense** is the one that costs you. Seed packed shoulder to shoulder holds moisture against itself, air stops moving through the canopy, and you get damping off, which is exactly the way [a tray goes wrong](/blog/when-a-tray-goes-wrong/) in a humid Florida shed. A tray that's slightly thin is a smaller harvest. A tray that's too dense is sometimes no harvest.

So the target is a surface where the seed is touching but not stacked. One layer, evenly, no bare patches and no piles.

That's a thing you can see. It's much harder to see a number.

Different seed makes the point better than an argument does. [Cilantro seed](/greens/cilantro/) is a big cracked husk and pea is bigger still, while [arugula](/greens/arugula/) is dust. Same tray, same target coverage, wildly different weights, and any single number you memorize is wrong for most of the varieties you grow.

The scale is still in the shed. It comes out for harvest, where the number matters because you're selling by the ounce and a customer is owed exactly what they paid for. Going in, the hand is fine.

If you're growing at home and you're new, weigh it the first few times. Then start guessing before you weigh, and check yourself. You'll be within tolerance in about a month and you'll never touch the scale again.
