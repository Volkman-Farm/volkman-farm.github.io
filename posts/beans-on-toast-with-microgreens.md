A can of white beans and a slice of good bread get you pretty far at lunch. Warm the beans with olive oil and a little salt, then mash some of them with the back of a fork so they stay on the toast. Leave a few whole if you like the texture.

White beans are mild enough that you don't need much on top to notice it. A few [radish microgreens](/greens/radish/) give them a sharp bite, especially if you didn't add much else to the pan. If you like the beans with lemon and black pepper already, try [pea shoots](/greens/pea/) instead. They're sweeter and give you something crisp against the soft beans.

Put the greens on after the toast is on your plate. If the pea shoots are long, cut them once or twice before you scatter them so they don't pull the beans off with the first bite. You don't need a separate salad to finish lunch, and you don't need to use half the box to find out if you like it.

If you're deciding [which greens to order](/greens/), think about what you'd usually put on beans. If you'd grab hot sauce, I'd start with radish. If you'd squeeze a lemon over them and call it done, I'd try pea.
