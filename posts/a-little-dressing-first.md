Put a little dressing on the greens, take a bite, and see what you think. You can always add more. It's harder to get it back out of the bowl.

If you're trying a microgreen for the first time, keep the dressing simple enough that you can taste the leaf. [Pea tendrils](/greens/pea/) have a sweet, grassy flavor. [Arugula](/greens/arugula/) brings pepper. A heavy pour of your strongest dressing can make those differences harder to notice.

Lemon juice and olive oil are an easy place to start. Mix them in a separate cup, then spoon a little over the portion you're eating. Toss gently and taste a forkful. Want it sharper? Add a little lemon to the cup. Too sharp? Add more oil, then try a little on the greens again.

Vinegar works too. Use one you already like, with oil, and leave the garlic, mustard, and sweetener out for the first bite. You may want them afterward. With peppery arugula or [mustard micros](/greens/mustard/), taste before adding mustard to the dressing as well.

For a creamy dressing, try a small spoonful of plain yogurt loosened with lemon juice. Add a little at a time so the greens get a light coating. If ranch is what you like, use ranch. Start with less than you'd pour over a big bowl of lettuce and see whether you want more.

Our clamshells say wash before using. Wash the portion you're about to eat under running water and dry it with a clean towel or paper towel. Keep the rest in the fridge. The [FDA's produce guide](https://www.fda.gov/food/buy-store-serve-safe-food/selecting-and-serving-produce-safely) covers that preparation step.

Dress the greens when you're ready to eat. We've covered [keeping dressing separate for a packed lunch](/blog/summer-salads-that-dont-wilt-by-noon/) and [adding greens to slaw](/blog/slaw-that-holds/). For tonight, a cup, a spoon, and a few bites will get you there.
