[True Leaf Market](https://www.trueleafmarket.com/). That's the list.

A post called "who we buy from and why" is supposed to have a comparison table in it, and a paragraph about agonizing over 3 vendors. We didn't. One supplier covers what we grow, the seed does what it's supposed to do, and there's been no reason to go looking.

We ask 2 things of it. Organic, and non-GMO. Both hold for [every variety on the rack](/greens/), and neither is negotiable in a crop where you eat the whole seedling about 8 days after it comes out of the bag.

## Seed is a different purchase here

In a garden you buy a packet. 20 seeds, maybe 30, a picture of a tomato on the front, and it lasts you a season or three.

In a shed you buy a sack.

One tray gets a scattering dense enough that the sprouts come up as a mat instead of as individuals, and that tray runs again every week or two, all year. Packet arithmetic falls apart in the first month. So seed stops being an afterthought and turns into the biggest input in the crop by weight.

It's also the only one you can't fix later. Bad soil you can amend. Bad light you can adjust. A hot shed you can [fight with a portable air conditioner](/blog/keeping-a-grow-shed-cool/). Seed that won't come up evenly is 2 weeks and a tray you don't get back.

## What we haven't solved

Whether we should buy differently at volume.

We're one shed with one rack. A grower our size either finds a second supplier for redundancy or gets caught out when something's not in stock, and we haven't had to deal with that yet. When we do, we'll write it up with the product named and the specifics on the page, the way we did with [the salad mix](/blog/whats-in-the-salad-mix/).

If you grow anything at home, go pull out whatever seed you have in the drawer and read the packet. Not the front. The back, where it says where the seed was grown and what it was treated with. Most people have never looked, and it's the same question we're answering here, just at packet scale.
