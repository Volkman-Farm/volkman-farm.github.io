Every grower who's started a tray of microgreens has bent down over it one morning and seen white fuzz.

Sometimes it's nothing. Sometimes the tray is done. I've stood over both in our shed, and the difference isn't hard to learn, but it's easy to panic before you've learned it.

We put a short version of this in [the home growing post](/blog/can-i-grow-microgreens-at-home/). It gets its own page here because a good tray is easy to throw out by mistake.

## Smell it first

Get your nose down near the soil.

A healthy tray smells like damp dirt and green growth. Mold smells musty, like a basement or a wet towel left in a bag. If a tray smells wrong, it usually is. Smell and look are the 2 things I go by.

## Then look at where it is and what shape it takes

**Root hairs** are fine, bright white, and fuzzy, almost like frost. They grow out of the roots and lower stems, close to the soil line, and they show up evenly across the whole tray at once, because every seedling is growing them at the same time. Each one belongs to a plant.

**Mold** is greyer and webbier. It spreads across the surface of the soil itself, between the stems and not just on them, and it starts as a patch. One corner, one spot, growing outward. Where root hairs are even, mold is lopsided.

If it's even, white, clinging to the roots, and the tray smells like dirt, leave it alone.

If it's grey, spreading in a patch, webbed across the soil, and it smells off, the tray's done. We don't cut around mold, and we wrote about why in [when a tray goes wrong](/blog/when-a-tray-goes-wrong/).

## When you can't tell

Wait a day. Root hairs pull back as the tray drinks and the roots find the soil. Mold doesn't pull back. It gets bigger.

If you're growing at home, next time you see fuzz, smell before you decide anything, then look for whether it's on the plants or on the dirt between them.
