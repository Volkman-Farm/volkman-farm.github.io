Start at 2 ounces.

That's our answer for almost everybody, and it's worth explaining rather than just asserting, because the instinct with something you're trying for the first time is to order a little and the instinct with something you like is to order a lot. Both of those go wrong in the same way.

An ounce is more than it sounds like. Microgreens are mostly air and structure, so an ounce is a real handful, enough to top 4 or 5 plates generously. 2 ounces covers a week of eggs, a couple of sandwiches, and a salad for a family without anybody rationing.

The reason not to go bigger is the clock. Cut greens [hold about 7 days in the fridge](/blog/how-long-do-microgreens-last/), and that's the number, not a conservative version of it. Order 6 ounces because you're enthusiastic and the last 2 are going in the trash on day 9. You didn't save money, you bought compost at $3.50 an ounce.

The other reason is that quantity is the easy thing to change. Everything runs [week to week with no contract](/blog/subscribe-and-save-how-the-10-percent-works/), so ordering 2 and then going to 4 costs you nothing but a text. Ordering 6 and discovering you eat 2 costs you a month of guilt about the drawer.

One thing that does shift the math: if you're buying for a household that already eats a salad most nights, 4 is the right starting point, not 2. You'll know which one you are.

Order 2 this week and pay attention to which day you run out. That day is your real answer, and it beats anything we'd guess for you.
