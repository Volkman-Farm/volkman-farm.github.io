Beet microgreens have pink stems. Pea tendrils curl. Both are easy to notice in a photograph, which gives them an advantage over a small green leaf that looks much like the next small green leaf.

Mustard doesn't need much help getting noticed once you eat it.

We talk about flavor on our [variety pages](/greens/) because the pictures can't tell you that part. [Beet](/greens/beet/) is earthy with a little sweetness. [Mustard](/greens/mustard/) has a sharp, horseradish-like bite. The color doesn't tell you which one you'll want more of.

There's nothing wrong with choosing food because it looks good. A few pink stems against pale cheese are pretty, and the curls on pea tendrils can make a sandwich look different before you've taken a bite. We like those details too. They just don't describe everything in the clamshell.

If you're looking at a photo and wondering about the greens, the variety name is useful. “Microgreens” could mean something mild, peppery, sweet or unmistakably cilantro. A picture of a finished dish might not give you enough information to order the same thing, even when the greens are right there in front of you.

That can work the other way too. [Broccoli microgreens](/greens/broccoli/) don't have the pink stems or the long curls, but their mild flavor may suit your lunch better. The less noticeable green in the picture can be the one you enjoy eating most.

We're happy to talk about that part. “I like the look of these, but I don't want anything peppery” gives us a useful place to start. You don't need to know all the varieties before asking.
