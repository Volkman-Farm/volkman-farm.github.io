A kale seed can end up 3 different ways, and you've probably bought all 3.

Cut it at 8 to 12 days and it's a microgreen. Cut it at a few weeks and it's baby kale, the loose leaves in a plastic tub. Leave it for 2 months or so and it's the tough bunch with a rubber band around it. Same seed. The only thing that changed was the calendar.

None of these words has a legal definition. They're what growers and grocers settled on, and the day counts drift depending on who's growing. But the stages are real, and each one eats differently.

## Micro

Days 7 to 21 on our rack, depending on the variety. The plant is a stem, 2 seed leaves, and sometimes the first true leaf [just starting to show](/blog/cotyledon-or-true-leaf/). We cut above the soil, and you eat the stem along with the leaves.

This is the stage where a plant tastes most like a concentrate of itself. Mustard is at [its sharpest](/blog/mustard-brings-the-heat/). Cilantro is louder than the bunch. And the texture is tender all the way through, because nothing has built fiber yet.

## Baby

Roughly 3 to 5 weeks. By now the plant has real leaves, several of them, and the stem has gone from something you eat to something you leave behind. Baby greens get cut as leaves only.

Flavor is milder than micro and more like the adult. Texture is somewhere in between: soft enough for a salad, sturdier than a microgreen. Baby spinach, baby kale, and most spring mix live here.

## Full size

Anywhere from 40 days to a few months. The leaf has done its job of feeding a growing plant, and it's thick, fibrous, and built to survive the sun. It's also where you get volume, which is why a full head of anything is cheap per pound and a clamshell of micros isn't.

For some plants the full-size version isn't a leaf at all. Grown broccoli is sold for its head, not its leaves. [Broccoli micros](/greens/broccoli/) are the only stage of that plant most people will ever eat as a green. Same with pea: the grown plant is for pods, and the [tendril](/greens/pea/) is its own thing entirely.

## Why the stage changes the plate

Heat is the big one. A full-size leaf can take a long braise. A baby leaf can take a quick wilt. A microgreen takes nothing, and goes on after the cooking stops.

Then use. Full size is a vegetable you build a dish around. Baby is a salad base. Micro is a finish, the thing you add last so a plate has a sharp note or a sweet one.

The next time you've got a clamshell of [our kale](/greens/kale/) in the fridge, pick up a tub of baby kale and a bunch of the grown stuff on your way home. Wash a leaf of each at the sink and taste them in order, small to big. You'll know which stage belongs in which dish by the third bite.
