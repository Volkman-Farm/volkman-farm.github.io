A tray of cilantro takes 18 to 21 days.

Thanksgiving is Thursday, Nov 26. Count back 3 weeks and that tray goes in around Nov 5, and the seed gets cracked before it's sown. So the holidays start for us in about a month, in the shed, long before anybody's thinking about a table.

Every green has its own clock. [Cilantro](/greens/cilantro/) is the slow one. [Kale](/greens/kale/) runs 8 to 12 days, pea tendrils 10 to 14, mustard 7 to 10. Whatever lands on your table in late November was decided by what went into soil in early or mid November, and we can only seed for what we know about.

That's where an order placed early earns its keep. A tray seeded on a guess is a bet. A tray we seed for an order is a tray we don't have to think about again until it's cut. If you'd want greens every week anyway, a [standing order](/subscribe/) is 10% off and tells us what to seed without a text each time. If you only want them for one dinner, an order in early November does the same job for that one week.

## The holiday weeks

The routes ride the family calendar, so the holidays move them a little.

**Thanksgiving week runs as usual.** Friday Nov 20 through Seminole County, Sunday Nov 22 downtown, Monday Nov 23 and Wednesday Nov 25 through north Orlando. Wednesday evening is the night before. A Friday clamshell is 6 days old by Thursday dinner, which is inside a microgreen's [week](/blog/how-long-do-microgreens-last/) but near the end of it, so plan to use those first.

**Christmas Day and New Year's Day are both Fridays, and we're skipping both.** No Seminole County route on Dec 25 or Jan 1. The last Friday run before Christmas is Dec 18. Sunday, Monday, and Wednesday run as usual both weeks.

If you're in Seminole County and cooking on the 25th, that Dec 18 clamshell will be at the tail of its week by dinner. Pick the sturdy ones (pea, kale, broccoli) and store them dry in the fridge. Wash the portion you're using right before it goes on the plate, same as any other week.

## Count it back yourself

Get a calendar. Mark the day you're cooking.

Count back to the last route day that reaches [your neighborhood](/delivery/) before it. Then count back again by the variety's days. If that second date has already passed, pick a faster green.
